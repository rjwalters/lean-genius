# `inverse-galois-d4-oq-03` — Problem statement

## Open question (parent: `inverse-galois-d4`)

> Is there a general criterion for when $\operatorname{Gal}(X^n - a / \mathbb{Q})$ is dihedral?

Here $a \in \mathbb{Q}^{\times}$, $n \ge 1$, and "dihedral" means the Galois group is isomorphic to a dihedral group $D_m$ of order $2m$ for some $m \ge 2$.

The parent gallery entry settles a specific instance: $n=4$, $a=2$ gives $\operatorname{Gal}(X^4-2/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$ of order $8$. The OQ-03 asks for a *general* criterion covering all $(n, a)$.

## Mathematical context

Throughout, write $K_n(a) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n, \sqrt[n]{a})$ for the splitting field of $X^n - a$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $G_n(a) = \operatorname{Gal}(K_n(a)/\mathbb{Q})$.

**General containment.** $G_n(a)$ embeds into the metacyclic semidirect product
$$ M_n = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \rtimes (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times} $$
via $\sqrt[n]{a} \mapsto \zeta_n^k \sqrt[n]{a}$ and $\zeta_n \mapsto \zeta_n^m$. So every Galois group of an $X^n - a$ is metacyclic.

**Capelli's irreducibility theorem (Capelli 1897).** $X^n - a$ is reducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ iff either
1. there exists a prime $p \mid n$ with $a \in (\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^p$, or
2. $4 \mid n$ and $a \in -4 (\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^4$.

Otherwise $X^n - a$ is irreducible and $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{a}):\mathbb{Q}] = n$.

**Generic order.** When $X^n - a$ is irreducible and $\sqrt[n]{a} \notin \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, the Galois group has order $|G_n(a)| = n \cdot \varphi(n)$, equal to the full metacyclic order. (Velez 1979, Acta Arith.)

## Why dihedral is restrictive

For $G_n(a)$ to be dihedral $D_m$ of order $2m$, we need:
- A cyclic normal subgroup of index $2$.
- Total order divisible by $2$ but tightly bounded: $|D_m| = 2m \le n \varphi(n)$.

Since the kernel of the projection $G_n(a) \to \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \le (\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}$ is cyclic (the rotation subgroup, generated by $\sqrt[n]{a} \mapsto \zeta_n \sqrt[n]{a}$), $G_n(a)$ is *always* a metacyclic extension of a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}$ by a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}/n$.

A finite metacyclic group is dihedral iff:
- The quotient acts on the cyclic kernel by inversion only, and
- The semidirect product has $\mathbb{Z}/2$ as quotient.

Equivalently: $|G_n(a)| = 2m$ and the cyclotomic-action subgroup $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q})$, restricted to its image in $\operatorname{Aut}(\langle \sqrt[n]{a} \rangle) = (\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}$, equals $\{1, -1\}$.

## Classical case analysis

**Case 1: $n$ odd, $n \ge 3$.** Then $-1 \in (\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}$ has order $2$, so dihedral is possible only when $|G_n(a)| = 2n$. For $X^n - a$ irreducible with $n$ odd prime, Velez shows $|G_n(a)| = n(n-1)$ generically; dihedral $D_n$ requires the cyclotomic action to collapse to $\{\pm 1\}$. This happens iff $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{a})$ already contains $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^{+}$ (the real subfield), forcing the action to be inversion. Such $a$ are rare; the canonical example for $n = 3$ is $a = $ rational cube of a unit in a quadratic field where this collapse occurs.

**Case 2: $n = 2k$ with $k$ odd.** Then $\zeta_n = -\zeta_k$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_k)$ of degree $\varphi(k)$. Generic order is $2k \varphi(k)$. Dihedral $D_{2k}$ requires order $4k$, so $\varphi(k) = 2$, i.e., $k \in \{3, 4, 6\}$, giving $n \in \{6, 8, 12\}$.

**Case 3: $n = 4$.** $(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\times} = \{1, 3\} = \{1, -1\}$, of order $2 = \varphi(4)$. For $X^4 - a$ irreducible (i.e., $a \notin (\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^2$ and $a \notin -4(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^4$), the generic order is $4 \cdot 2 = 8$, giving $D_4$. The parent gallery entry's $a = 2$ is a representative.

**Case 4: $n = 8$.** $(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$, order $4 = \varphi(8)$. Generic order is $32$, far from any $D_m$. Dihedral requires drastic collapse — e.g., $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[8]{a})$ already contains a subfield killing two of the three nontrivial elements of $(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times}$.

**Case 5: $n$ a power of an odd prime.** Cyclotomic order $\varphi(p^k) = p^{k-1}(p-1)$. Generic Galois order $p^k \cdot p^{k-1}(p-1)$. Dihedral requires the cyclotomic part to reduce to $\{\pm 1\}$ — i.e., the kernel of $(\mathbb{Z}/p^k)^{\times} \twoheadrightarrow \{\pm 1\}$ to lie in the subgroup fixing $\sqrt[n]{a}$ — a strong condition.

## Schinzel–Velez classification (informal)

Combining the cases above, the answer to OQ-03 (in the Schinzel–Velez program) is:

$\operatorname{Gal}(X^n - a / \mathbb{Q}) \cong D_m$ **iff** all three of the following hold:
1. $X^n - a$ has irreducible factorization $X^n - a = \prod_{d \mid n} f_d$ where each $f_d$ is determined by Capelli's theorem;
2. The maximal degree of an irreducible factor equals $m$;
3. The Galois action on the $m$-th cyclotomic part collapses to $\{\pm 1\} \subset (\mathbb{Z}/m)^{\times}$.

Concretely:
- For $n$ a prime $p \ge 3$: $X^p - a$ has Galois group $D_p$ iff $a \in -p (\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^p$, where $p$ is a specific element of the real subfield — a sporadic condition rarely satisfied for rational $a$.
- For $n = 4$ with $a > 0$, $a$ not a rational square: $D_4$ generically.
- For $n = 4$ with $a < 0$, $a \ne -4b^4$: also $D_4$ generically (but the explicit $D_4$ generators differ).
- For $n = 6$ with appropriate $a$: $D_6$ in select cases; generally $G \cong S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ or larger.

## What this OQ asks for

Operationally, OQ-03 asks: provide a Lean-formalizable predicate $\mathrm{IsDihedralGaloisGroup}(n, a)$ that:
1. Computes, given $n \in \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}$ and $a \in \mathbb{Q}^{\times}$, whether $G_n(a) \cong D_m$ for some $m$.
2. Returns the order $m$ when dihedral.
3. Is *decidable* in the sense that the computation reduces to finite arithmetic checks (Capelli irreducibility + cyclotomic order checks).

The expected answer is a finite-case characterization keyed on $n \bmod 8$ and the $p$-adic valuations of $a$.
