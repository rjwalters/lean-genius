# Problem: Non-Derogatory Matrices Characterized by Cyclic Vectors

## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

$$
M \in \mathrm{M}_n(K) \text{ is non-derogatory} \iff \exists\, v,\ \{v, Mv, M^2v, \dots, M^{n-1}v\}
\text{ is a basis of } K^n,
$$
where non-derogatory means the minimal polynomial equals the characteristic polynomial
($\deg \mu_M = n$).

### Plain Language

A matrix is *non-derogatory* when its minimal polynomial has the maximum possible degree $n$
(equivalently, minimal = characteristic polynomial). We want to prove this is equivalent to the
existence of a *cyclic vector*: a vector $v$ whose iterates $v, Mv, \dots, M^{n-1}v$ span the whole
space. Such a $v$ makes $M$ similar to a single companion matrix.

### Why This Matters

The cyclic-vector characterization is the conceptual bridge from the abstract minimal-polynomial
condition (parent oq-02) to the concrete rational canonical / companion-matrix form (sibling oq-01):
a non-derogatory matrix is exactly one similar to a single companion block. Formalizing it supplies a
reusable criterion for "$M$ is determined up to similarity by its characteristic polynomial" and
underpins module-theoretic structure results $K[X]/(\mu_M) \cong K^n$.

## Known Results

### What's Already Proven

- Minimal polynomial divides characteristic polynomial; $\mu_M = \chi_M$ characterization of non-derogatory matrices — parent `cayley-hamilton-reduction-oq-02` (verified).
- Cayley–Hamilton theorem and `Matrix.charpoly` API — Mathlib.
- `Module.AnnihilatingPolynomial`, `minpoly`, and cyclic-module (`Module.IsCyclic`-style) infrastructure in Mathlib.

### What's Still Open

- The biconditional between non-derogatory and existence of a cyclic vector, in Lean.
- The forward direction (non-derogatory $\Rightarrow$ cyclic vector exists) is the substantive part.

### Our Goal

Prove $\deg \mu_M = n \iff \exists v,\ \{M^i v\}_{i<n}$ is a basis. The easy direction: a cyclic vector
forces $\deg \mu_M \ge n$, hence $= n$. The hard direction: when $\mu_M = \chi_M$, view $K^n$ as a
$K[X]$-module via $M$; it is cyclic (generated by one element) precisely because the module is
$K[X]/(\mu_M)$, and a generator gives the cyclic vector.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Approach A**: Module-theoretic — view $K^n$ as $K[X]$-module $M_\bullet$ where $X$ acts as $M$.
   - Why it might work: $K^n \cong \bigoplus K[X]/(f_i)$ (invariant factors); non-derogatory $\iff$ a single invariant factor $\iff$ cyclic module $\iff$ cyclic vector generator.
   - Risk: Mathlib's structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID may need substantial glue for the matrix action.

2. **Approach B**: Direct linear-algebra — show $\{v,Mv,\dots\}$ independent iff $v$ avoids all proper $M$-invariant subspaces containing lower-degree annihilators.
   - Why it might work: constructive; picks $v$ from a generalized-eigenspace decomposition.
   - Risk: requires splitting field / careful handling over a general field $K$.

### Key Difficulties

- Transferring between the matrix `M` and the induced `K[X]`-module action in Mathlib's `Module` framework.
- The forward direction needs existence of a generator, which relies on structure-of-modules-over-PID machinery.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Key lemma 1: the $K[X]$-module induced by $M$ is cyclic $\iff \deg\mu_M = n$.
- Key lemma 2: a module generator corresponds to a vector $v$ with $\{M^iv\}_{i<n}$ a basis.
- Technical requirements: `minpoly`, `Matrix.charpoly`, `Module.IsCyclic` / PID structure theorem, `LinearMap` powers.
