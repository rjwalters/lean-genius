# Problem: Cluster-Point Form of Compactness from the Finite-Intersection Property

**Created**: 2026-06-24
**Status**: Active
**Source**: gallery-gap

## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

$$
K \text{ compact} \implies \text{every net } (x_\alpha) \text{ in } K \text{ has a cluster point in } K,
$$
derived from the finite-intersection-property (FIP) dual of the open-cover definition, applied to the closures of the net's tails $T_\alpha = \overline{\{x_\beta : \beta \geq \alpha\}}$.

### Plain Language

The standard definition of compactness is "every open cover has a finite subcover." Dualizing gives the finite-intersection property: any family of closed sets with the finite-intersection property has nonempty total intersection. The goal is to use *this* form to prove the cluster-point characterization: every net (or filter) on a compact set clusters somewhere. The tails of a net form a descending family of closed sets with the FIP, so their intersection — the cluster points — is nonempty.

### Why This Matters

The cluster-point / net characterization is the bridge between covering compactness and sequential/limit-point compactness, and it is the form most used in functional analysis (e.g., weak-* compactness via nets). Deriving it directly from FIP — without invoking Mathlib's filter-based `IsCompact` lemmas — exhibits the logical core of compactness from first principles. The exercise is specifically to avoid citing Mathlib's high-level filter compactness API and instead build the equivalence from the FIP statement proved in the parent line.

Prove: for a compact set $K$ (via FIP), the family of closed tails of any net in $K$ has the finite-intersection property, hence nonempty intersection, and any point therein is a cluster point.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Tails-as-closed-FIP-family**: Show the closures of tails are closed, descending, each nonempty, hence FIP; apply the parent FIP statement to get a common point.
   - Why it might work: tails are directed-decreasing, so FIP is immediate.
   - Risk: cluster-point definition bookkeeping (frequently-in vs. eventually-in).

2. **Filter form**: Phrase via the filter generated by tails and prove a cluster point exists.
   - Why it might work: aligns with Mathlib's `ClusterPt`.
   - Risk: must avoid the very lemmas we are reproving.

### Key Difficulties

- Stating "cluster point of a net" without leaning on Mathlib's filter compactness lemmas.
- Ensuring closures of tails remain inside considerations of $K$ correctly.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Key lemma: descending family of nonempty closed sets has the FIP.
- Key lemma: $x \in \bigcap_\alpha \overline{T_\alpha} \iff x$ is a cluster point of the net.
- Technical requirements: net/filter tail machinery, closure properties.
