# Problem: Norm Equations in Number Fields of Degree > 2

**Slug**: pell-equation-oq-05
**Created**: 2026-06-14
**Status**: Active (OBSERVE)
**Source**: gallery-gap (parent: `pell-equation`)

## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

Pell's equation $x^2 - D y^2 = 1$ is the norm-one equation $N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)/\mathbb{Q}}(x+y\sqrt D)=1$
for the real quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$. Its solution structure is governed by
Dirichlet's unit theorem: the unit group has rank $1$, so solutions form a cyclic group generated
by the fundamental unit. This problem asks for the **higher-degree analogue**: for a number field
$K$ of degree $n>2$, describe the solutions of the norm equation

$$
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\xi) = m, \qquad \xi \in \mathcal{O}_K,
$$

and in particular the structure of the unit group ($m=1$) via Dirichlet's unit theorem
$\mathcal{O}_K^\times \cong \mu_K \times \mathbb{Z}^{r_1 + r_2 - 1}$.

### Plain Language

Pell's equation is "find integer points on a hyperbola"; the answer is that they form a single
chain generated by one fundamental solution. The generalization replaces $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$ by
a bigger number field (e.g. a cubic field): now the units have rank $r_1+r_2-1$, so solutions are
organized by *several* fundamental units, and the norm equation $N(\xi)=m$ has finitely many
solution classes up to units.

### Why This Matters

This is the bridge from a single, beloved Diophantine equation to the structural heart of
algebraic number theory (Dirichlet's unit theorem, regulators, $S$-unit equations). Mathlib has
substantial algebraic-number-theory infrastructure (`NumberField`, `RingOfIntegers`, and a unit
theorem). Connecting the gallery's concrete Pell entry to that machinery — stating and proving
the rank formula and the finiteness of norm-equation solution classes — is a high-value
formalization that turns an isolated example into an instance of a general theorem.

## Known Results

### What's Already Proven

- `pell-equation` — solution structure for $x^2-Dy^2=1$ (parent), tied to the fundamental unit.
- Mathlib: `Pell` namespace (special $D$), `NumberField.Units.rank` and Dirichlet's unit theorem (`NumberField.Units.exists_...`), `RingOfIntegers`, `Algebra.norm`.

### What's Still Open (in this gallery)

- A general statement: $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ has rank $r_1+r_2-1$, recovering Pell's rank-1 case for real quadratics.
- Finiteness of solution classes of $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\xi)=m$ modulo units, for a fixed $m$.

### Our Goal

Formalize, for a concrete cubic field (e.g. $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]2)$), the unit-group rank and the
description of $N(\xi)=1$ solutions via fundamental units, and state the general finiteness of
$N(\xi)=m$ solution classes — reusing Mathlib's Dirichlet unit theorem rather than reproving it.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Instantiate Mathlib's unit theorem (recommended)**: apply `NumberField.Units` rank results to
   a concrete cubic, extract the fundamental unit(s), and describe $N(\xi)=1$.
   - Why it might work: the deep theorem (Dirichlet) is already in Mathlib; the work is specialization and packaging.
   - Risk: computing an explicit fundamental unit / regulator for a specific field can be fiddly.

2. **$S$-unit / finiteness route for $N(\xi)=m$**: derive finiteness of solution classes from finiteness of the class group and units.
   - Why it might work: aligns with Mathlib's class-group and unit infrastructure.
   - Risk: assembling the finiteness-of-classes statement may need bridging lemmas not yet present.

### Key Difficulties

- Moving from the abstract rank theorem to an *explicit* generating set for a chosen field.
- Stating the norm-equation finiteness cleanly modulo the unit-group action.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Key lemma 1: rank $= r_1+r_2-1$ specialized and shown to recover Pell ($r_1=2,r_2=0 \Rightarrow$ rank $1$).
- Key lemma 2: $\{\xi : N(\xi)=m\}/\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ is finite.
- Technical requirements: `NumberField.Units`, `Algebra.norm`, `ClassGroup`, `RingOfIntegers`.
