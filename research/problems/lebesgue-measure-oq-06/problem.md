# Problem: Lebesgue Measure: Banach-Tarski Paradox Formal Statement in Lean

## Statement

### Plain Language

The Banach-Tarski paradox (1924) states that a solid unit ball in ℝ³ can be decomposed into
finitely many disjoint pieces that can be rearranged (using only rigid motions — rotations and
translations) into two solid unit balls of the same size. This "doubling" is possible only
because the pieces are non-measurable: they cannot be assigned a Lebesgue measure.

The research goal is to formally state the Banach-Tarski paradox in Lean 4, building on
the existing `lebesgue-measure` gallery proof and Mathlib's group theory infrastructure.
The formal statement requires: defining finitely-many non-measurable pieces of B³, defining
rigid motions via SO(3) ⋊ ℝ³, and asserting that the image of the pieces covers two unit balls.

This is a statement-level formalization task (not a full proof): the Hausdorff paradox and
free subgroup construction require deep Axiom of Choice arguments; the goal is to crystallize
what the statement looks like in type theory and identify the key missing Mathlib components.

### Formal Statement

$$
\exists k \in \mathbb{N},\ \exists A_1, \ldots, A_k \subseteq B^3 \text{ (disjoint, covering } B^3),\
\exists g_1, \ldots, g_k \in \mathrm{SE}(3)
$$
$$
\text{such that } g_1(A_1) \sqcup \cdots \sqcup g_k(A_k) = B^3 \sqcup B^3
$$

where $B^3 = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \|x\| \leq 1\}$ is the closed unit ball and
$\mathrm{SE}(3) = \mathrm{SO}(3) \ltimes \mathbb{R}^3$ is the group of rigid motions.

Equivalently, via Tarski's theorem on paradoxical decompositions: $B^3$ is **paradoxically
decomposable** with respect to the isometry group.

## Why This Matters

1. **Measure theory foundation**: Banach-Tarski is the canonical demonstration that the
   restriction of Lebesgue measure to σ-algebras is mathematically necessary, not a
   technical convenience. A Lean formalization crystallizes this conceptually.
2. **Non-measurable sets in type theory**: Formalization forces precision about what
   "non-measurable" means in Lean's type theory (sets in `Set (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3))`
   that are not `MeasurableSet`).
3. **Group theory connection**: The proof uses the free subgroup of SO(3) generated by two
   specific rotations — a deep algebraic fact. Formalizing the statement identifies what
   group-theoretic infrastructure Mathlib needs.
4. **Axiom of Choice usage**: The decomposition pieces require AC to construct. The formal
   statement reveals exactly where AC enters and whether it can be replaced by weaker
   choice principles.
5. **Gallery completion**: The existing `lebesgue-measure` gallery entry explicitly lists
   Banach-Tarski as an open question — this problem directly addresses that gap.

## Known Obstacles

- **SO(3) in Mathlib**: Mathlib has `Matrix.SpecialOrthogonalGroup` but the API for
  actions on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)` may need bridging lemmas.
- **Free subgroup in SO(3)**: The Hausdorff paradox (1914) constructs a free group
  on 2 generators inside SO(3). This requires specific rotation matrices (e.g., rotation
  by `arccos(1/3)` around different axes) and verifying they generate a free group —
  a non-trivial algebraic computation.
- **Non-measurability**: The pieces of the decomposition are necessarily non-measurable
  (if they were measurable, measure additivity would give a contradiction). Lean's type
  system doesn't "know" a set is non-measurable unless proved.
- **Axiom of Choice**: The Vitali-type selection argument requires AC. Lean's `Classical.choice`
  can provide this, but the proof complexity is high.
