## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

$$
K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2},\, i) \text{ is the splitting field of } X^4 - 2 \text{ over } \mathbb{Q}, \quad [K : \mathbb{Q}] = 8, \quad \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4.
$$

### Plain Language

The parent entry proves that $\sqrt[4]{2}$ has degree exactly 4 over $\mathbb{Q}$, but $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})$ is a *real* field and so contains only two of the four complex roots of $X^4 - 2$ (namely $\pm\sqrt[4]{2}$); the other two, $\pm i\sqrt[4]{2}$, are non-real. The smallest field in which $X^4 - 2$ factors completely — its splitting field, i.e. the Galois closure of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})$ — is obtained by additionally adjoining $i$. We want to prove that this field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$, that it has degree 8 over $\mathbb{Q}$, and that its group of $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphisms is the dihedral group of order 8, $D_4$ — the symmetry group of a square, generated by a rotation $r: \sqrt[4]{2}\mapsto i\sqrt[4]{2}$ (order 4) and a reflection $s$ (complex conjugation, order 2) with $srs = r^{-1}$.

### Why This Matters

This is the canonical first non-abelian Galois group worked out in every introductory Galois-theory course: $X^4 - 2$ is *the* textbook example of a quartic whose splitting field is non-abelian and whose lattice of intermediate fields realizes the full subgroup lattice of $D_4$. Formalizing the explicit isomorphism $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$ — not merely the order of the group — supplies a reusable template for computing concrete Galois groups in Lean, and connects the gallery's degree/irreducibility results (the parent) to its inverse-Galois realizations (which establish only the cardinality $|\operatorname{Gal}| = 8$). It also exercises the part of Mathlib's Galois API that is genuinely thin: building a named group isomorphism rather than counting automorphisms.

## Known Results

### What's Already Proven

- $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}):\mathbb{Q}] = 4$ via $X^4 - 2$ irreducible (Eisenstein at 2 + Gauss) — parent entry `fourth-root-2-irrational` (`Proofs/FourthRoot2Degree4.lean`).
- $|\operatorname{Gal}(X^4 - 2 / \mathbb{Q})| = 8$, realizing $D_4$ as a Galois group over $\mathbb{Q}$ (lower bound $4 \mid |\operatorname{Gal}|$ from $\deg = 4$; upper bound via $X^2 + 1$ has no root in $\operatorname{AdjoinRoot}(X^4 - 2)$) — gallery entry `inverse-galois-d4`.
- General Galois machinery in Mathlib: `IsGalois.card_aut_eq_finrank` (automorphism count equals degree for a finite Galois extension), `Polynomial.IsSplittingField`, `Polynomial.Gal` (the Galois group of a polynomial), `DihedralGroup` — Mathlib field/group theory.

### What's Still Open

- A *structural* (named group-isomorphism) statement $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{DihedralGroup}\ 4$, beyond the cardinality $|\operatorname{Gal}| = 8$ that `inverse-galois-d4` already gives.
- An explicit identification of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ as `IsSplittingField` of $X^4 - 2$, together with the intermediate-field lattice corresponding to the subgroup lattice of $D_4$.

### Our Goal

Establish the three-part statement: (1) $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ is the splitting field of $X^4 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$; (2) $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 8$ via the tower $\mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ with degrees $4$ and $2$; and (3) a concrete multiplicative isomorphism $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{DihedralGroup}\ 4$ given by sending the order-4 generator $r$ to $\sqrt[4]{2}\mapsto i\sqrt[4]{2},\ i\mapsto i$ and the reflection $s$ to complex conjugation. The isomorphism — not just the order count — is the new contribution over `inverse-galois-d4`.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Approach A** — Tower + generator construction: prove $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ is a splitting field, get $[K:\mathbb{Q}]=8$ from the tower law ($i \notin \mathbb{R} \supseteq \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})$ so $X^2+1$ stays irreducible there), then build the isomorphism by hand: define $r, s \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ by their action on the two generators $\sqrt[4]{2}$ and $i$, verify $r^4 = s^2 = 1$ and $srs = r^{-1}$, and exhibit `DihedralGroup 4 ≃* Gal` via `MonoidHom` out of the dihedral presentation, injective+order-8 hence iso.
   - Why it might work: each automorphism of $K$ is determined by where it sends $\sqrt[4]{2}$ (4 choices, the 4 roots) and $i$ (2 choices), giving exactly the 8 dihedral elements; the generator actions are explicit.
   - Risk: Mathlib has no ready "define an automorphism by its values on generators" combinator; pinning down well-definedness and the relation $srs = r^{-1}$ on the splitting-field representation is fiddly.

2. **Approach B** — Reuse `inverse-galois-d4` cardinality + classify: take $|\operatorname{Gal}| = 8$ as given, prove the group is non-abelian (two automorphisms that fail to commute) and has an element of order 4, then invoke a classification of order-8 groups to conclude $\cong D_4$ (the only non-abelian order-8 group with a single involution-pair pattern... actually $Q_8$ is the other non-abelian order-8 group).
   - Why it might work: reduces the bespoke construction to a finite group-theory fact.
   - Risk: Mathlib does not have a packaged "order-8 group classification"; distinguishing $D_4$ from the quaternion group $Q_8$ requires counting elements of order 2 (D_4 has 5 involutions, Q_8 has 1), which is itself a nontrivial computation — likely no easier than Approach A.

### Key Difficulties

- Constructing a *named* group isomorphism to `DihedralGroup 4` is the crux: Mathlib's Galois API is strong on `Fintype.card`/`finrank` equalities but offers little support for matching $\operatorname{Gal}$ to a specific abstract group; the homomorphism and its bijectivity must be assembled manually.
- Specifying field automorphisms by their action on generators $\sqrt[4]{2}, i$ and proving well-definedness (that the assignment respects the defining relations of $K$) without a high-level combinator.
- Choosing a tractable model of $K$ (concrete subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ via `IntermediateField.adjoin` vs. abstract `SplittingField`/`AdjoinRoot`) so that both the degree computation and the explicit automorphism actions are workable in the same representation.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Key lemma 1: $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ is a splitting field of $X^4 - 2$ — all four roots $\{\pm\sqrt[4]{2}, \pm i\sqrt[4]{2}\}$ lie in $K$ and generate it (`Polynomial.IsSplittingField`).
- Key lemma 2: $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 8$ via the tower law `IntermediateField.finrank_mul_finrank` (or `Module.finrank` multiplicativity), with $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]=4$ (parent) and $[K:\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})]=2$ ($X^2+1$ irreducible over the real field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})$).
- Technical requirements: the explicit generators $r$ (order 4) and $s = $ complex conjugation (order 2), the relation $srs = r^{-1}$, and a `MulEquiv` `Gal (X^4 - 2) ≃* DihedralGroup 4`; `IsGalois` instance for $K/\mathbb{Q}$ (normal + separable, automatic in char 0 for a splitting field).

## Tractability Assessment

**Difficulty**: Medium

**Justification**:
- This is a classical, fully-understood computation (every Galois-theory textbook), so there is no mathematical uncertainty — the work is entirely formalization engineering.
- A strong head start exists in-gallery: the parent supplies the degree-4 base and `inverse-galois-d4` already proves $|\operatorname{Gal}| = 8$ over the *same* polynomial, so the splitting-field/degree half is largely a matter of reassembly.
- Mathlib provides splitting fields, `IsGalois`, `IsGalois.card_aut_eq_finrank`, `Polynomial.Gal`, and `DihedralGroup`; the genuinely effortful part is the bespoke `MulEquiv` to `DihedralGroup 4`, where library support is thin — this is what pushes it from Low to Medium rather than a one-session exercise.

**Estimated Effort**:
- Exploration: 1-2 days (settle on the field model; locate the right tower-law and splitting-field lemmas; draft the generator actions).
- If tractable: 1-2 weeks (degree-8 splitting field + the explicit $D_4$ isomorphism).
- If hard: the splitting-field degree-8 statement is near-certain; if the named isomorphism proves too costly, fall back to delivering $\operatorname{Gal}$ non-abelian + element of order 4 + the explicit generator descriptions as a partial result.
