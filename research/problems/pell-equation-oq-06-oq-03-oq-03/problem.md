# Problem: Uniform Trace Recurrence for Negative Pell Solution Chains

**Status**: Active

## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

Fix a nonsquare $D > 0$ for which the negative Pell equation
$x^2 - D y^2 = -1$ is solvable (equivalently, $\sqrt{D}$ has an odd
continued-fraction period). Let $(x_1, y_1)$ be the fundamental solution of the
**positive** Pell equation $x^2 - D y^2 = +1$, and let
$(a_n, b_n)_{n \ge 0}$ enumerate the negative-Pell solution chain (the odd powers
of the fundamental unit of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{D}]$). Prove that **each coordinate
sequence** $u_n \in \{a_n, b_n\}$ satisfies the second-order linear recurrence

$$
u_{n+2} = 2x_1\, u_{n+1} - u_n,
$$

with the trace coefficient $2x_1$ depending only on $D$ through the fundamental
positive unit, uniformly in $D$. (The parent entry proves the concrete case
$D = 2$: $u_{n+2} = 6u_{n+1} - u_n$, where $x_1 = 3$.)

### Plain Language

Solutions of the negative Pell equation come as a chain generated by multiplying
by the fundamental unit $\varepsilon = x_1 + y_1\sqrt{D}$. Because $\varepsilon$
satisfies its own quadratic $\varepsilon^2 = 2x_1\,\varepsilon - 1$ (its
characteristic/minimal polynomial has trace $2x_1$ and norm $1$), **every**
integer sequence built from powers of $\varepsilon$ — in particular both the $x$-
and $y$-coordinates of the solution chain — obeys the same linear recurrence
$u_{n+2} = 2x_1 u_{n+1} - u_n$. The generalization replaces the hard-coded $6$ of
the $D=2$ case with the uniform trace $2x_1$.

### Why This Matters

It exposes the Cayley–Hamilton mechanism behind Pell solution chains: the
recurrence coefficient is the trace of the companion matrix of the fundamental
unit. This unifies the per-$D$ recurrences into one theorem and connects Pell
theory to linear recurrences / Lucas sequences.

### What's Still Open

- The uniform-in-$D$ statement with coefficient $2x_1$ as a named theorem.
- Identifying the trace $2x_1$ with $\varepsilon + \bar\varepsilon$ (the field
  trace of the fundamental unit) inside `Zsqrtd`.

### Our Goal

State and prove $u_{n+2} = 2x_1 u_{n+1} - u_n$ for the coordinates of the
solvable negative-Pell chain, deriving the coefficient as the trace of the
fundamental positive unit via $\varepsilon^2 = 2x_1\varepsilon - 1$.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Fundamental-unit power identity** (primary): work in `Zsqrtd D`; set
   $\varepsilon = \langle x_1, y_1\rangle$ with norm $+1$; prove
   $\varepsilon^2 = 2x_1\varepsilon - 1$ (from $\varepsilon\bar\varepsilon = 1$
   and $\varepsilon + \bar\varepsilon = 2x_1$); multiply the chain relation
   $s_{n+1} = \varepsilon\, s_n$ (or odd-power step) and read off coordinates.
   - Why it might work: the recurrence is literally the minimal polynomial of
     $\varepsilon$ applied to the sequence; coordinatewise projection is linear.
   - Risk: aligning "odd powers only" indexing of the negative chain with the
     step map (the negative chain uses $\varepsilon^{2n+1}$).

2. **Companion-matrix / Cayley–Hamilton**: encode the step as multiplication by
   the $2\times2$ companion matrix of $X^2 - 2x_1 X + 1$; C–H gives the
   recurrence for all matrix entries, hence all coordinates.
   - Why it might work: mechanical once the matrix is set up.
   - Risk: more machinery than the direct `Zsqrtd` computation.

### Key Difficulties

- Correct handling of the odd-power indexing of the *negative* Pell chain versus
  the all-powers chain of the positive equation.
- Existence/solvability hypothesis: the theorem is conditional on $D$ being a
  solvable negative-Pell modulus; state this hypothesis cleanly rather than
  proving the continued-fraction criterion.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Lemma: $\varepsilon + \bar\varepsilon = 2x_1$ and $\varepsilon\bar\varepsilon
  = 1$ for the fundamental positive unit in `Zsqrtd D`.
- Lemma: $\varepsilon^2 = 2x_1\varepsilon - 1$ (minimal polynomial).
- Lemma: coordinate projections $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle \mapsto x, y$ are
  additive, so the recurrence transfers to each coordinate.
