### S5 finding: `InnerProductSpace.ofNorm` exists

`Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/OfNorm.lean` contains
`InnerProductSpace.ofNorm`, which constructs an inner product on a
normed space from the **parallelogram identity**:
`‖x + y‖² + ‖x - y‖² = 2 (‖x‖² + ‖y‖²)`.

The polarization is given explicitly:
`⟨x, y⟩ := (1/4) (‖x + y‖² - ‖x - y‖² + i ‖i x + y‖² - i ‖i x - y‖²)`,
and the proof verifies conjugate-symmetry, additivity, scalar-linearity
(via ℕ → ℤ → ℚ → ℝ → ℂ extension), all conditional on the parallelogram
identity.

This was already in v4.26.0; what S5 adds is the recognition that it
lets us **factor** the option-B path:

### Crit-PI vs crit-IPS

Old crit-IPS: "any IPS structure on A from NormedDivisionRing".
New crit-PI: "parallelogram identity on A from NormedDivisionRing".

These are **equivalent**: `InnerProductSpace ↔ parallelogram identity`
(both directions are in Mathlib). The benefit of crit-PI is that it
pins down the **mathematical statement** rather than the type-class
machinery — a future Mathlib contributor knows exactly what file to put
the lemma in and what signature to prove.

**Important caveat**: crit-PI is *not* meaningfully weaker than crit-F.
The parallelogram identity for a finite-dim NDR over ℝ implies:
1. The norm comes from an inner product (via `ofNorm`).
2. Combined with norm-multiplicativity (`‖xy‖ = ‖x‖·‖y‖`), this forces
   the multiplication to be a "composition algebra structure" on a real
   inner product space.
3. Hurwitz's classical 1898 theorem (the very sorry we're trying to
   close!) then gives dim ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}. For *associative*
   composition algebras (NDR is associative), the octonion case is
   excluded, leaving dim ∈ {1, 2, 4} — i.e., Frobenius.

So crit-PI is a different *route* to the same result, not a shortcut.
But it suggests a cleaner Mathlib-side strategy: a contributor who
proves crit-PI directly (via the imaginary-subspace argument from
Frobenius's 1878 paper) can then pipe through `ofNorm` to get an inner
product, and either build out Hurwitz from there (closing this sorry)
or go via Wedderburn.
