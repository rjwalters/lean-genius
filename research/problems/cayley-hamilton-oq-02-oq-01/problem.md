## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

Let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ (the roots of the
characteristic polynomial $\chi_A$, listed with multiplicity in a fixed order). Define the
matrix "partial products"

$$
\rho_0 = I, \qquad \rho_k = (A - \lambda_k I)\,\rho_{k-1} = \prod_{i=1}^{k}(A - \lambda_i I)
\quad (1 \le k \le n),
$$

and the scalar functions $P_1, \dots, P_n : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$ defined as the unique
solution of the triangular linear ODE system

$$
\dot P_1 = \lambda_1 P_1, \quad P_1(0) = 1, \qquad
\dot P_k = \lambda_k P_k + P_{k-1}, \quad P_k(0) = 0 \quad (2 \le k \le n).
$$

Then the matrix exponential is

$$
e^{tA} \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{n} P_k(t)\,\rho_{k-1}.
$$

The formalization goal is a Lean 4 theorem of the shape

```
theorem putzer_matrix_exp {n : ℕ} (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ)
    (lam : Fin n → ℂ) (hlam : A.charpoly = ∏ i, (X - C (lam i)))
    (P : Fin n → ℝ → ℂ) (hP : /- the triangular ODE system with initial data above -/) :
    ∀ t : ℝ, NormedSpace.exp ℝ (t • A) = ∑ k, (P k t) • (ρ A lam k)
```

where `ρ A lam k = ∏ i ∈ Finset.range k, (A - (lam i) • 1)`.

### Plain Language

Putzer's algorithm computes $e^{tA}$ — the fundamental solution of the linear system
$\dot x = Ax$ — **without diagonalizing $A$ or computing any eigenvectors**. It needs only the
eigenvalues (roots of the characteristic polynomial). The recipe: form the running products
$\rho_k$ of the factors $(A - \lambda_i I)$, solve a simple triangular chain of scalar first-order
ODEs for the coefficient functions $P_k(t)$ (each is an elementary integral of exponentials), and
assemble $e^{tA} = \sum_k P_k(t)\rho_{k-1}$. Because $\rho_n = \chi_A(A) = 0$ by
Cayley–Hamilton, the sum truncates at $n$ terms and always lies in the algebra generated by $A$.

### Why This Matters

- **Constructive matrix exponential without Jordan form.** The standard closed forms for $e^{tA}$
  go through diagonalization or the Jordan canonical form, both of which require eigenvectors and
  are numerically and formally delicate (repeated eigenvalues, generalized eigenspaces). Putzer's
  method sidesteps all of this: it works uniformly for repeated eigenvalues and never leaves the
  commutative subalgebra $\mathbb{C}[A]$.
- **Bridges algebra and analysis.** It is the concrete analytic payoff of the purely algebraic
  reduction proved in the parent entry `cayley-hamilton-oq-02`: matrix functions are polynomials of
  degree $< n$ in $A$. Putzer supplies the *time-dependent coefficients* of that degree-$<n$
  polynomial.
- **Foundational for linear ODEs and control theory.** Solutions of $\dot x = Ax,\ x(0)=x_0$ are
  $x(t) = e^{tA}x_0$; Putzer gives them in closed form from the characteristic polynomial alone.

## Known Results

### What's Already Proven

- **Cayley–Hamilton (`Matrix.aeval_self_charpoly`)** — `aeval M M.charpoly = 0`, giving
  $\rho_n = \prod_i (A-\lambda_i I) = \chi_A(A) = 0$ over any commutative ring
  (`Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.Charpoly.Basic:211`).
- **Matrix exponential (`NormedSpace.exp` on matrices)** — definition and algebra of $e^{A}$,
  including `exp_add_of_commute`, `exp_neg`, `exp_conj`, `exp_diagonal`
  (`Mathlib.Analysis.Normed.Algebra.MatrixExponential`).
- **Derivative of exp (`hasDerivAt_exp`, `hasFDerivAt_exp`)** — `HasDerivAt (exp 𝕂) (exp 𝕂 x) x`
  over a normed field of characteristic zero (`Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Exponential:197`).
- **ODE uniqueness (`ODE_solution_unique`, `ODE_solution_unique_univ`)** — two solutions of
  $\dot y = v(t,y)$ with a Lipschitz right-hand side and equal initial data agree
  (`Mathlib.Analysis.ODE.Gronwall:379`).
- **Polynomial reduction mod $\chi_A$** — the parent entry `cayley-hamilton-oq-02` proves
  `aeval A p = aeval A (p %ₘ charpoly A)` and partial-sum reduction, the algebraic scaffolding for
  writing $e^{tA}$ as a degree-$<n$ polynomial in $A$.

### What's Still Open

- No formalization of the Putzer recursion $\rho_k$, the triangular $P_k$ ODE system, or the
  assembled identity $e^{tA} = \sum_k P_k(t)\rho_{k-1}$ exists in Mathlib or the gallery.
- No general "the matrix-valued map $t \mapsto e^{tA}$ is *the* solution of $\dot M = AM$,
  $M(0)=I$" wrapper specialized so that `ODE_solution_unique` can be applied to matrix-valued
  functions $\mathbb{R} \to M_n(\mathbb{C})$ (Mathlib's ODE uniqueness is stated for a general
  normed space $E$, which $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ is, but the plumbing is not packaged).

### Our Goal

Formalize the Putzer identity for a fixed eigenvalue listing: define $\rho_k$ and the ODE-defined
$P_k$, prove that $M(t) := \sum_{k=1}^n P_k(t)\,\rho_{k-1}$ satisfies $\dot M = AM$ and $M(0) = I$,
and conclude $M(t) = e^{tA}$ by uniqueness of the matrix-valued linear ODE solution. The scope is
the complex case with eigenvalues supplied as a splitting of $\chi_A$ (so we may assume
$\chi_A = \prod_i (X - \lambda_i)$).

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Approach A — ODE uniqueness (recommended).**
   Define $\rho_k$ by the given recursion and $P_k$ as the analytic solutions of the triangular
   system. Set $M(t) = \sum_{k=1}^n P_k(t)\rho_{k-1}$. Differentiate term-by-term and use the
   *key algebraic identity* $A\,\rho_{k-1} = \rho_k + \lambda_k \rho_{k-1}$ (from
   $\rho_k = (A-\lambda_k I)\rho_{k-1}$) together with $\dot P_k = \lambda_k P_k + P_{k-1}$ to show
   $\dot M = AM$; the boundary term $\rho_n$ vanishes by Cayley–Hamilton. Check $M(0) = P_1(0)\rho_0 = I$.
   Then $t \mapsto e^{tA}$ solves the same linear matrix ODE $\dot M = AM$, $M(0)=I$, so by
   `ODE_solution_unique` (the RHS $M \mapsto AM$ is globally Lipschitz with constant $\|A\|$),
   $M(t) = e^{tA}$.
   - Why it might work: every ingredient (matrix exp derivative, Lipschitz linear vector field,
     Grönwall uniqueness, Cayley–Hamilton) is already in Mathlib.
   - Risk: packaging `HasDerivAt` for a `Finset.sum` of products of matrix-valued functions, and
     matching Mathlib's `HasDerivWithinAt ... (Ici t)` interval form.

2. **Approach B — direct exponential-series manipulation.**
   Expand $e^{tA} = \sum_m t^m A^m/m!$, reduce each $A^m$ modulo $\chi_A$ via the parent entry's
   `partial_sum_reduces`, and identify the resulting coefficient of $\rho_{k-1}$ with $P_k(t)$ by
   solving the induced recurrence.
   - Why it might work: reuses the already-verified reduction machinery; avoids ODE theory.
   - Risk: interchanging the infinite sum with the finite reduction requires a uniform-convergence
     argument, and re-deriving that the coefficients coincide with the ODE solutions is itself the
     hard combinatorial identity.

### Key Difficulties

- Applying `ODE_solution_unique` to *matrix-valued* functions: instantiating the general normed
  space $E = M_n(\mathbb{C})$ and proving the vector field $M \mapsto A\cdot M$ is Lipschitz
  (constant $\|A\|_{op}$) and that $t \mapsto e^{tA}$ has the required `HasDerivWithinAt` on
  $[a,b]$.
- Establishing $\dot M = AM$ requires the telescoping/index-shift identity between
  $A\rho_{k-1} = \rho_k + \lambda_k\rho_{k-1}$ and $\dot P_k = \lambda_k P_k + P_{k-1}$, plus the
  vanishing boundary term $P_n(t)\rho_n = 0$ from Cayley–Hamilton — bookkeeping over `Fin n`
  with `Finset.sum_range_succ` is error-prone.
- Constructing the scalar $P_k$ and their derivatives: they can be written explicitly as iterated
  convolutions of exponentials, but proving `HasDerivAt (P k) (λ k * P k t + P (k-1) t) t` cleanly
  may require either an explicit formula or a bespoke Picard–Lindelöf existence step.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Key lemma 1: `rho_succ : A * ρ k = ρ (k+1) + (lam k) • ρ k` (rewriting the recursion) and
  `rho_top : ρ n = 0` (Cayley–Hamilton, from `aeval_self_charpoly`).
- Key lemma 2: `hasDerivAt_M : HasDerivAt (fun t => ∑ k, P k t • ρ (k-1)) (A * M t) t`, assembled
  from `HasDerivAt.sum`, `HasDerivAt.smul`, and the $P_k$ derivative hypotheses.
- Key lemma 3: `matrix_exp_hasDerivAt : HasDerivAt (fun t => exp ℝ (t • A)) (A * exp ℝ (t • A)) t`
  (chain rule through `hasDerivAt_exp` and commutation $A\cdot e^{tA} = e^{tA}\cdot A$).
- Technical requirements: a Lipschitz-continuity witness for $M \mapsto AM$ on $M_n(\mathbb{C})$;
  `NormedSpace.exp ℝ` with the local operator-norm instance for matrices; adaptation of
  `ODE_solution_unique` to the matrix-valued setting.

## Tractability Assessment

**Difficulty**: Medium | High

**Justification**:
- Every conceptual ingredient exists in Mathlib: matrix exponential (`MatrixExponential`), its
  derivative (`hasDerivAt_exp`), Cayley–Hamilton (`aeval_self_charpoly`), ODE uniqueness
  (`ODE_solution_unique`), and the algebraic reduction (parent entry `cayley-hamilton-oq-02`).
- The strategy (build a candidate solution, verify $\dot M = AM$/$M(0)=I$, invoke uniqueness) is a
  standard, well-trodden formalization pattern that has succeeded for scalar ODEs in Mathlib.
- The risk is concentrated in *plumbing* rather than mathematics: instantiating the vector-space
  ODE machinery for matrices, handling `Fin n` index bookkeeping in the derivative of the sum, and
  constructing the scalar $P_k$ with their derivatives. None of these is a research-level obstacle,
  but together they make this a substantial multi-lemma development rather than a quick corollary.

**Estimated Effort**:
- Exploration: 2–4 days (map the matrix `NormedSpace.exp` derivative and the matrix-valued
  instantiation of `ODE_solution_unique`; prototype `rho_succ` and `rho_top`).
- If tractable: 2–3 weeks (full `putzer_matrix_exp` with the $\dot M = AM$ verification, the $P_k$
  construction, and the uniqueness step).
- If hard: unknown (if the matrix-valued ODE-uniqueness plumbing or the $P_k$ existence proof does
  not go through cleanly, the analytic layer could balloon; the algebraic core would still ship as a
  standalone contribution).
