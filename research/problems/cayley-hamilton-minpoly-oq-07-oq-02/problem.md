# Problem: The Matrix Inverse as a Polynomial over a Commutative Ring

**Slug**: cayley-hamilton-minpoly-oq-07-oq-02
**Created**: 2026-06-30
**Status**: Active
**Source**: gallery-gap

## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

Let `R` be a commutative ring, `n` a finite index type, and `A : Matrix n n R`
with `IsUnit (A.det)`. Let `p = charpoly A`, `c₀ = p.coeff 0`. Define the
inverse polynomial

$$
r \;=\; C\bigl(\mathrm{Ring.inverse}(-c_0)\bigr)\cdot \operatorname{divX}(p)\ \in\ R[X].
$$

Then:

$$
A^{-1} \;=\; (\mathrm{aeval}\,A)\,r
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A^{-1} \in R[A] = \operatorname{Algebra.adjoin} R\{A\},
$$

with `r.natDegree < Fintype.card n` (assuming `R` nontrivial and `n` nonempty).

Equivalently: whenever the determinant of `A` is a **unit** of `R` (not merely
nonzero), the inverse `A⁻¹ = Ring.inverse A.det • adjugate A` coincides with the
evaluation at `A` of an explicit polynomial of degree `< n`, so the inverse lies
in the commutative subalgebra generated by `A`.

The precise upgrade over the parent: replace the hypothesis `det A ≠ 0` (which
only characterizes invertibility over a field) by `IsUnit (det A)`, and replace
the field inverse `(-c₀)⁻¹` by `Ring.inverse (-c₀)`.

### Plain Language

The parent entry showed that over a *field*, the inverse of an invertible matrix
is a polynomial in the matrix itself (a rearrangement of Cayley–Hamilton). Over a
general commutative ring, "invertible" is a stronger condition than "nonzero
determinant": we need `det A` to be an actual **unit** of the ring. Under that
correct hypothesis the entire argument still goes through — Cayley–Hamilton, the
constant term `c₀ = ±det A`, and the division step — provided we divide by the
*unit* `−c₀` using `Ring.inverse` rather than a field reciprocal. The goal is to
restate and reprove the parent's results at this generality.

### Why This Matters

- **Correct level of generality.** The field hypothesis `det A ≠ 0` is exactly
  the special case of `IsUnit (det A)` in which nonzero elements are
  automatically units. Stating the result for commutative rings pins down what
  actually drives the proof: invertibility of the constant term of the
  characteristic polynomial.
- **Downstream applicability.** Matrices over `ℤ`, over polynomial rings `R[X]`,
  over `ℤ/mℤ`, and over local rings appear routinely (integer/unimodular
  matrices with `det = ±1`, matrices over `p`-adic integers). For these, "nonzero
  determinant" is *not* enough; "unit determinant" is the right and necessary
  condition, and the inverse-as-polynomial statement is exactly what one wants
  for constructive inversion (Faddeev–LeVerrier / Cayley–Hamilton based
  inversion) in those settings.
- **Mathlib alignment.** Mathlib already builds `Matrix.nonsing_inv`,
  `adjugate`, and Cayley–Hamilton over commutative rings; this entry connects the
  polynomial-in-`A` viewpoint to that native generality.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Near-mechanical typeclass port (recommended).** Copy the parent file and
   make three coordinated substitutions:
   - `variable {R : Type*} [CommRing R]` in place of the field;
   - `noncomputable def invPoly (A) := C (Ring.inverse (-(A.charpoly.coeff 0))) * A.charpoly.divX`;
   - replace `charpoly_coeff_zero_ne_zero : det A ≠ 0 → coeff 0 ≠ 0` by
     `charpoly_coeff_zero_isUnit : IsUnit A.det → IsUnit (A.charpoly.coeff 0)`,
     obtained from `det A = (-1)^n · c₀` and the fact that `(-1)^n` is a unit
     (`IsUnit.mul_iff`, extract the second factor).

   In `aeval_invPoly_mul_self`, the only genuine change is the cancellation:
   the field step `inv_mul_cancel₀` becomes `Ring.inverse_mul_cancel _ hunit`
   where `hunit : IsUnit (-(A.charpoly.coeff 0))` (from `IsUnit.neg` of the unit
   `c₀`). The `smul`/`algebraMap` bookkeeping (`aeval_divX_mul_self`,
   `Algebra.algebraMap_eq_smul_one`, `smul_mul_assoc`, `smul_smul`) is
   ring-generic and ports verbatim. `inv_eq_left_inv`, `mul_eq_one_comm`,
   `adjoin_singleton_eq_range_aeval`, and the degree lemmas already hold over
   commutative rings, so those theorems copy over with only the hypothesis name
   changed. **Risk: low.**

2. **Derive the ring case from the already-proven field case** via a ring
   homomorphism into a field. Not recommended — there is no natural target field,
   and this loses the `Ring.inverse` explicitness that is the point.

3. **Bypass Cayley–Hamilton, use the adjugate formula directly**
   (`A⁻¹ = Ring.inverse det • adjugate A`). Not recommended: it proves
   invertibility but not the *polynomial-in-A* content, which is the whole point
   of the entry.

### Key Difficulties

- **Field reciprocal → ring unit inverse.** The single conceptual change. Use
  `Ring.inverse`; its cancellation lemmas (`Ring.inverse_mul_cancel`,
  `Ring.mul_inverse_cancel`) require an `IsUnit` witness, so the unit hypothesis
  must be threaded to the cancellation step (unlike the field case where `x ≠ 0`
  suffices definitionally).
- **Extracting `IsUnit c₀` from `IsUnit (det A)`.** Need that `(-1)^(card n)` is
  a unit and peel it off the product `det A = (-1)^n · c₀`. `IsUnit.mul_iff`
  (commutative monoid) handles this; confirm the exact lemma name.
- **`Nontrivial R` for the degree bound.** `charpoly_natDegree_eq_dim` needs a
  nontrivial ring (over the zero ring `card n` collapses); add `[Nontrivial R]`
  to `invPoly_natDegree_lt` and `exists_inv_eq_aeval` only.
- Everything else (`inv_eq_left_inv`, `mul_eq_one_comm`, adjoin range, `divX`
  degree lemmas) is already at comm-ring generality — the main hazard is simply
  confirming that and not silently over-assuming a field somewhere.

### What Would a Proof Need?

- `charpoly_coeff_zero_isUnit (A) (hA : IsUnit A.det) : IsUnit (A.charpoly.coeff 0)`.
- `aeval_divX_mul_self` — verbatim port (already ring-generic; uses
  `aeval_self_charpoly`, `divX_mul_X_add`, `map_add`/`map_mul`/`aeval_X`/`aeval_C`).
- `aeval_invPoly_mul_self (A) (hA : IsUnit A.det) : (aeval A) (invPoly A) * A = 1`
  — the one theorem whose internals change (Ring.inverse cancellation).
- `inv_eq_aeval_invPoly` via `Matrix.inv_eq_left_inv`.
- `invPoly_natDegree_lt` (needs `[Nontrivial R] [Nonempty n]`).
- `inv_mem_adjoin`, `self_mul_aeval_invPoly` (`mul_eq_one_comm`),
  `exists_inv_eq_aeval` — verbatim ports with the new hypothesis.

## Tractability Assessment

**Difficulty**: Low

**Justification**:
- This is a genuine but modest generalization — essentially a typeclass-weakening
  exercise. The parent's proof already avoids field-specific tactics almost
  everywhere.
- The sole substantive edit is swapping the field reciprocal `(-c₀)⁻¹` for
  `Ring.inverse (-c₀)` and carrying an `IsUnit` witness to the cancellation
  lemma; the `IsUnit c₀`-from-`IsUnit det` extraction is a two-line argument.
- All supporting Mathlib lemmas (Cayley–Hamilton, `det_eq_sign_charpoly_coeff`,
  `inv_eq_left_inv`, `mul_eq_one_comm`, `divX` degree lemmas, adjoin range) are
  already stated over commutative rings.
- Honest split: roughly 90% mechanical port; the `Ring.inverse` cancellation and
  `IsUnit c₀` extraction are the only new content. The real value is stating the
  *correct* hypothesis (`IsUnit det`, not `det ≠ 0`).

**Estimated Effort**:
- Exploration: 1–2 hours.
- If tractable (expected): ≈130–160 lines, 8 theorems + 1 definition, mirroring
  the parent's structure.
- If hard: only risk is an unexpected field assumption buried in a Mathlib lemma
  signature — then substitute the comm-ring analogue.
