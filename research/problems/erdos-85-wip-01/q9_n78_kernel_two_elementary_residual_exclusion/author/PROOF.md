# The elementary-abelian center image cannot have residual orbits 8+16

Assume a simple C4-free nine-regular graph on78 vertices has full automorphism group A of order16, the matching normal form2419, S-orbits2+4, and kernel K of order2 on S. Assume specifically the image on S is the product C2 x V4: the first factor exchanges S2, and V4 acts regularly on S4. Thus A/K is elementary abelian of order8. We exclude the residual orbit profile R=X8 union Y16; the other residual profile is not excluded here.

Write K=<z>. It is central, since a normal subgroup of order2 is central. All commutators and squares belong to K. Every conjugacy class therefore has size at most2. A central involution cannot stabilize a vertex outside S: its fixed set would contain that entire A-orbit, of size at least8, contradicting the six-fixed bound2257. In particular, the stabilizer of any outside eight-orbit is generated by a noncentral involution. Its conjugacy class has size2, its centralizer has size8, and it fixes exactly |C_A(g)|/2=4 points on its eight-orbit.

All W-orbits are regular of size16. Indeed, suppose a W vertex w had a nontrivial stabilizer g. It fixes the unique S-neighbor of w. If this is in S2, then g lies in its stabilizer H, which acts freely outside S by2419, a contradiction. If it is in S4, the product image fixes all four S4 points: a nonidentity translation of the regular V4 action has no fixed point. Together with the four fixed points on the W eight-orbit, this gives at least eight fixed vertices, again impossible. Thus W consists of three regular sixteen-orbits W_i.

In the assumed R=X8 union Y16 profile, the involutory stabilizer of x in X acts freely on Y, so x has either zero or two Y-neighbors. Its internal X degree is accordingly three or one. The elementary cubic-eight obstruction proved in2425 excludes degree three: in a C4-free cubic graph on eight vertices each vertex must lie in exactly one triangle, which would partition eight vertices into triangles. Therefore X has internal degree one and two Y-neighbors; balance gives each Y vertex one X-neighbor and internal Y degree two. This argument uses only the orbit sizes, regularity and cubic residual degree, not the faithful-action hypothesis elsewhere in2425.

Let b_i be the Y-degree of a vertex in W_i. Equal orbit sizes make this also the W_i-degree of a Y vertex. The quotient2419 gives 0<=b_i<=3 and sum b_i=6. If all b_i=2, every vertex outside S has exactly two Y-neighbors (including X and Y themselves). Fix y in Y. None of its nine neighbors lies in S, and each supplies only one other Y endpoint of a length-two path. At most nine of the other fifteen Y vertices therefore share a neighbor with y. At least six have zero codegree with y. This contradicts the global zero-codegree degree five: the nine neighbors of any vertex supply 9*8=72 distinct nonreturning two-step endpoints by C4-freeness, leaving exactly 77-72=5 other vertices with no common neighbor.

Hence the b_i are not all two. For three integers of sum six, sum binomial(b_i,2) is at least four unless all are two: sum (b_i-2)^2 is a positive even integer, and sum binomial(b_i,2)=3+sum (b_i-2)^2/2. Apply2438's free-middle unordered-pair lemma to each regular W_i and to the regular orbit Y itself. Their pair images are mutually disjoint by C4-freeness. They require at least four free A-orbits of unordered Y pairs from W, plus one from internal Y degree two. A regular A-set has one free unordered-pair orbit for each inverse pair of noninvolutions. Thus A must have at least ten elements of order greater than two.

We finish with an elementary quadratic-form argument showing this impossible for a central extension of an elementary abelian group of order8 that has a noncentral involution. Put V=A/K, identified with F2^3. Define q(v) in F2 by a^2=z^{q(v)} for a lift a of v; this is independent of the lift. Define beta(v,w) by [a,b]=z^{beta(v,w)}. Centrality of commutators gives a bilinear alternating form beta, with q(v+w)=q(v)+q(w)+beta(v,w). A lift is central exactly when its image lies in the radical of beta.

If beta=0, A is abelian, contradicting the noncentral involution supplied by X. Otherwise the alternating form on the three-dimensional space has rank two and a one-dimensional radical L. This follows directly by choosing e,f with beta(e,f)=1, then splitting off their nondegenerate plane; its one-dimensional orthogonal complement is the radical.

Let l be the nonzero element of L. If q(l)=1, translation by l exchanges the zero and one values of q, so exactly four elements of V have q=1. Each has two lifts of order4, giving exactly eight noninvolutions. If q(l)=0, q descends to the nondegenerate two-dimensional quotient V/L. On a symplectic basis its nonzero values are a,b,a+b+1. It therefore has either one or three nonzero vectors with value1. In the first case A has four noninvolutions. In the second case the only zeros of q in V lie in L, so every involution of A is central, again contradicting X. These exhaust the cases. Whenever a noncentral involution exists, A has at most eight noninvolutions, contrary to the required ten.

Consequently the K2, image C2 x V4 case can only have residual orbit sizes8+8+8. This does not exclude those actions, the D8 graph images, full order16, or the global graph problem. No finite search, capped-domain retry or Lean formalization is used. Independent review is requested.
