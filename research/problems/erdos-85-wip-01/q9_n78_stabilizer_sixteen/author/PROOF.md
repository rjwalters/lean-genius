# Necessary equality form for an N78 vertex-fixing two-group of order16

Assume the graph hypotheses and accepted2257, and let H have order16 and fix a vertex v. Then H fixes a neighbor x of v and acts faithfully and transitively on Omega_v=N(v) minus{x}, of size eight. The same is true at x, with Omega_x=N(x) minus{v}. The edge vx lies in no triangle. The point-stabilizer involutions on these two eight-point actions form two disjoint H-conjugacy classes, each of size four. In particular H has at least nine nonidentity involutions, counting a central involution separately.

To prove transitivity, choose a central involution z. The second centralizer case of2257 would bound |H| by four, so z must be free on Omega_v. In the first case equality16 forces a kernel of order two and image of order eight in S4, acting on the four z-pairs. An order-eight subgroup of S4 is transitive: an intransitive subgroup embeds in products of symmetric groups on a proper partition of four, whose orders have two-part at most four. Since z itself swaps each pair, H is transitive on all eight points. This also shows that v's only H-fixed neighbor is x.

The same2257 argument applies to H fixing x, with v as its fixed neighbor. Thus the analogous eight-point action is faithful and transitive as well. If x were adjacent to any point of Omega_v, invariance and transitivity would make it adjacent to all eight. Any two of these points would then have the distinct common neighbors v and x, giving C4. Therefore vx has no common neighbor.

Choose a in Omega_v. Its H-stabilizer has order two by orbit-stabilizer; write it as {1,t}. By2257 every nonidentity involution has zero or two fixed points in this action, so t has exactly two. In the transitive coset action H/<t>, the number of fixed points of t equals |C_H(t)|/2: a coset h<t> is fixed exactly when h^-1 t h=t, and each coset has two representatives. Consequently |C_H(t)|=4, and the conjugacy class T_v of t has size four. Transitivity shows that precisely the involutions in T_v fix any point of Omega_v. Similarly the action at x yields a conjugacy class T_x of size four.

These two classes are disjoint. Otherwise an involution in their intersection fixes v,x and two further neighbors at each endpoint. In its fixed graph, both v and x have degree three. The fixed set must have six vertices: fewer than four cannot accommodate degree three, and with four vertices two adjacent degree-three vertices have two common neighbors and hence C4. The accepted six-fixed classification leaves3K2 or a triangle with one pendant leaf per centre. Only the latter has degree-three vertices, and any two adjacent degree-three vertices lie on its triangle. This contradicts the fact that vx lies in no triangle of G.

Finally a central involution z cannot fix any point of either faithful transitive eight-point action: if it fixes one, centrality makes it fix every point, violating faithfulness. Thus z lies in neither T_v nor T_x, giving at least4+4+1=9 nonidentity involutions.

This is a necessary equality form, not an exclusion of order16 stabilizers, a classification of H, or a reduction of the accepted global bound32. No search or Lean formalization is used. The graph premises and finite centralizer estimates are those of2257.
