# No six-vertex orbit of internal degree two when |Aut(G)|=24

Let G be a simple C4-free nine-regular graph on78 vertices with full automorphism group A of order24. Suppose an A orbit F has size6 and G[F] is two-regular. We show this is impossible.

## The F action is faithful

Its kernel K lies in a vertex stabilizer of order24/6=4. If nontrivial, K contains an involution fixing all six vertices of F. Accepted2257 bounds the fixed set of an involution by six and classifies the induced graph when equality holds as3K2 or a triangle with one pendant leaf on each triangle vertex. Neither is two-regular. Thus K is trivial, embedding A faithfully into Aut(G[F]).

A simple two-regular graph is a union of cycles of length at least3. On six vertices the only possibilities are C6 and2K3. The cycle has automorphism group of order12, which cannot contain a subgroup of order24. It remains to rule out a faithful transitive order24 subgroup of Aut(2K3).

## The two-triangle group has no such subgroup

Let M=Aut(2K3)=(S3 times S3) semidirect C2, where the last factor swaps the triangles. Thus |M|=72. The rotations N=C3 times C3 form a normal subgroup of order9, with quotient of order8. For any subgroup A of order24, L=A intersect N has order3: its order divides gcd(24,9)=3, and the quotient A/L embeds in M/N, so L cannot be trivial.

L is normal in A. If A acts transitively on six vertices, all L orbits have equal size, because elements of A send L orbits to L orbits. Since L is a nontrivial faithful subgroup of permutations, it cannot fix every vertex. Its orbits therefore all have size3. In particular a generator acts as a nontrivial rotation on each triangle.

Write the two triangle rotations as r1,r2. After choosing a generator of L, it is generated by (r1,r2^epsilon), where epsilon is1 or-1. Every element of M acts by independently inverting the two rotation coordinates and possibly swapping them; the nine rotations themselves centralize N. The line spanned by(1,epsilon) is preserved exactly when the two inversion signs agree. Swapping coordinates preserves either such line because epsilon^2=1. Hence the normalizer of L in M has order

    9 rotations * 2 equal-inversion choices * 2 triangle-swap choices =36.

But normality of L in A implies A is contained in this normalizer. Lagrange's theorem would require24 to divide36, a contradiction. Thus the two-triangle case is impossible as well.

This proof also explains why merely observing24 divides72 would be insufficient: the normal3 subgroup and its normalizer eliminate the putative embedding.
