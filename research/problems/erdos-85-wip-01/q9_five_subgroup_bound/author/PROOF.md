# Five-subgroups of hypothetical q9 automorphism groups

Assume G is a finite C4-free graph of minimum degree at least9 and order N=78 or80. Its automorphism group A is finite because it is a group of permutations of a finite vertex set.

At N78, accepted paper review2166 excludes an automorphism of exact order5. Cauchy's theorem therefore implies5 does not divide |A|.

At N80, accepted2166 says every automorphism of exact order5 is fixed-point-free. Let P be any subgroup of A whose order is a power of5. For any vertex v, its stabilizer P_v is a subgroup of P and hence has power-of-five order. If P_v were nontrivial, Cauchy would give an element of order5 fixing v, a contradiction. Thus every vertex stabilizer in P is trivial. Orbit-stabilizer implies each P-orbit has |P| vertices, so |P| divides80. The only powers of5 dividing80 are1 and5. Hence every5-subgroup has order at most5. Applying this to a Sylow5 subgroup shows25 does not divide |A|.

In particular there is no element of order25 (or of order divisible by25) at either order. Combining with the accepted large-prime and order-seven exclusions2163/2169 gives |A|=2^a*3^b at N78, and |A|=2^a*3^b*5^e with e=0 or1 at N80, for nonnegative integers a,b. This does not bound a or b, exclude trivial automorphism groups, or establish existence/nonexistence of G. It does not exclude all mixed cyclic actions of order10/20: those may have orbit lengths5/10/20 and are not necessarily free actions of the full cyclic group.

This is a paper group-action corollary of accepted2166, not an independent derivation of the order5 fixed-point theorem or a Lean theorem. No search or computation.
