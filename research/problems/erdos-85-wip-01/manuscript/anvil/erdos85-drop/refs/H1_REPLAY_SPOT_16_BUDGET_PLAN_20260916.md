# H1 replay: 16-box spot budget and launch plan

Prepared 2026-09-16 for goal #41, from the frozen [fleet specification](H1_REPLAY_SHARDED_FLEET_SPEC.md) and its [2026-09-10 cost model](h1_replay_fleet_costs_20260910.json). This is a plan for the editor's launch journal, not a launch authorization or a claim that its prelaunch gates have passed. No cloud resource was launched for this plan.

## Scope and duration

Start with **16 `r7g.2xlarge` spot hosts**, one Lean replay process per host, 300 GB gp3 each, in `us-east-1`, using 16 immutable shards and the original one-row pilot as a separate legacy shard. The target for this wave is the **12,019 historical ready subtotal**, subject to a fresh eligible-object and identity freeze. A key's existence is insufficient: each row must pass the queue builder's capacity, table, CNF, terminal and hash joins. The full 13,351-slot closure has **1,288 reported certificate gaps** in the separate capacity-grid census, and this replay wave cannot close those gaps. A fresh inventory may change both counts.

The original count-only model gives 4,114.5 box-hours, or **10.71 days** at 16 hosts before spot losses. The more conservative byte-weighted model gives 4,831.4 compile box-hours. Applying the spec's 25% noncompile overhead, an additional 10% spot loss, and eight aggregate bootstrap hours gives about **6,651 box-hours / 17.32 days** and **$1,276 replay infrastructure** at the historical spot price. This is the planning duration; the room's 11–13 day estimate uses the count-only model. Large-leaf memory, imperfect shard balance, spot interruptions and unresolved input gaps can extend it. Recompute from the frozen size-weighted shards and the four-job Lean rehearsal before launch.

## Daily cash ceiling

The operator's limit is **strictly below $100 per calendar day across all clouds and services**, not $100 for this fleet alone. The editor owns the common ledger. Use both UTC and `America/Los_Angeles` day windows and enforce the more restrictive remaining allowance, because the operator did not specify a billing timezone. Carry already accrued charges from other paper work into both windows. An empty or delayed cross-service ledger blocks new dispatches.

At the 2026-09-10 observed `us-east-1b` spot price of **$0.1535 per host-hour**, 300 GB gp3 at $0.08/GB-month and public IPv4 at $0.005/hour imply **$0.191833 per host-hour**, or **$73.664/day for 16 continuously running hosts**. The sampled $0.1722 spot price yields **$80.845/day**. These are historical sensitivity values, not a current quote or a total daily budget. Refresh spot price, AZ capacity, gp3 shape, public IPv4, S3 and other service rates at launch. The 16-host plan uses 128 of the historically available 300 Standard Spot vCPUs; check current free quota after producer usage.

Reserve at least **$20/day** for S3 retrieval, requests, new output storage and other measured paper spending, and **$5/day** for metering lag and price movement. Thus admit 16 hosts only if a conservative rolling 24-hour forecast plus accrued spend is **under $75/day** for the fleet and **under $95/day total**; the $100 limit is a hard stop, not a target. At the historical $0.1535 spot price, the host line uses $73.664 and leaves $1.336 in the $75 fleet allocation. At $0.1722, scale down: 14 hosts cost about $70.74/day, while 15 cost about $75.79/day. The editor may allocate more than $75 to replay only by reducing other spend and documenting a new forecast that remains below $95 total.

Glacier Instant Retrieval is charged by bytes read, not by completed jobs. At $0.03/GB, a **$20 retrieval allowance covers at most 666 decimal GB/day**, before S3 request or monitoring charges. The spec's 6.06 TB historical input subtotal would cost about $182 per full post-transition read; repeat validation and materialization can multiply it. The launch journal must choose either a reviewed temporary transition suspension with a restoration record, or measured same-day validation/materialization with daily read throttles. Do not assume all consumed objects remain Standard: transition depends on original object age. Pause retrieval before its next admitted batch can exceed the day's remaining allowance. New output storage and later aggregate hardware need separate measured allocations.

## Ledger and automatic stop

Maintain one append-only editor journal with `(time UTC, service/account, run id, instance id, shard id, event, units, unit-price quote, estimated USD, source receipt)` for launches, terminations, EBS volumes, public IP hours, S3 retrieval bytes, requests, output bytes and any other active cloud spend. Reconcile with provider billing when it arrives, but **do not use delayed Cost Explorer data as the dispatch safety signal**. Every 15 minutes, and immediately before replacing or adding a host, compute for both day windows:

`accrued_upper_bound + running_host_rate * hours_to_next_check + reserved_inflight_S3 + other_service_forecast`.

Accrued upper bound uses observed running seconds rounded upward to billable units and the greater of the current quoted spot rate or the last observed billed rate. Inflight S3 reservation uses the full known byte size of every scheduled read multiplied by its possible retrieval rate, including retries and validator/materializer rereads. If a service cannot provide a defensible upper bound, stop new work until it can. Record the forecast and source snapshots in each journal tick. Reconcile estimates to bills daily; carry any positive variance into the next day's reserve.

At **$90 forecast**, stop scheduling and new downloads, reject replacements, and begin an orderly drain of active jobs. At **$95 forecast**, terminate idle hosts and pause active shards at their next safe transaction boundary; if the forecast cannot remain below $100 while they finish, terminate the hosts and resume later from immutable ready/receipt records. At **$100 actual or forecast**, terminate the fleet immediately and notify the editor/operator. Spot price or concurrent-service spend that makes a 16-host forecast fail the $75/$95 admission test reduces N before any new dispatch. The editor can also issue an immediate manual stop; the journal records the command and host termination confirmation. Static shard ownership remains fixed across a pause; a replacement cannot start until the prior instance is authoritatively terminal.

## Prelaunch evidence required in the journal

1. Freeze a reconciled eligible set, exact capacity ordinal/index and hashes, accepted-pilot evidence, current object sizes, deterministic 16-way partition, shard queues/manifests, overlay/source/toolchain identities, and unique output prefixes. Prove exact union and no duplicate tag, slot or input key; preserve the 1,288 unresolved slots as a distinct ledger. Validate every old receipt against its original manifest.
2. Complete the fleet spec's implementation work: filtered-index freezer, reviewed multi-manifest merger/materialization v2, bounded validated scratch cleanup, conditional multipart upload and immutable readback. Review all affected source and pin a new freight revision.
3. Run and independently review the specified four **real Lean** jobs across two two-job shards, including largest available input, nested Lean peak RSS, disk high-water mark, stop/resume, duplicate-dispatch rejection, corruption rejection, and merged materialization. If the largest leaf exceeds the 48 GiB gate, freeze a separately costed 128 GiB heavy lane and revise N/quotas before launch.
4. Verify current Standard Spot quota/free usage, AZ spot prices and capacity, instance role/IAM, network route without unbudgeted NAT, EBS provision and disposal, S3 lifecycle state, daily cross-service spend, and the ledger/kill-switch dry run. Show the editor's journal binding each shard to one live instance and its bootstrap hash. Refresh the time/cost range from frozen byte weights and the rehearsal.
5. Launch only the admitted host count. Review the first production shard's receipt and spend tick before filling remaining slots. Stop on any proof, hash, axiom, quota, lifecycle, memory or budget anomaly. Complete independent receipt validation, exact 13,351-slot coverage, aggregate cold build and axiom audit before changing the manuscript's certificate-closure marker.

The 12,019 replayable-object wave and the 1,288 missing certificates are distinct work. The latter require separately scoped proof-logged solves or recovered certificates, then another frozen replay wave; this plan does not price or authorize their production.
