## major

- **§1 What the paper does not claim [L66] — "Every number in the paper traces to a banked receipt named in Section~\ref{sec:artifacts}."** False on its face. The Artifacts paragraph (§6) names the census table, the gap audit, the cube tree check and the cold axiom audit. The cost and projection figures of §4.5–4.6 (12,019; 4,831; 6,651; 17.32 d; \$1,276; 6.06 TB; \$182; 574; 4,011 s; 4,539 s; 7,102 s; 22; 5.5 GB/h; 509 MB / 984 s; 2,704; 2,996; 121; 6; 14.9 TB; 6,700; 360; 4 TB; \$16; \$2,000–2,500) trace to the replay budget plan, the v3 timing readback, the fleet-cost JSON, the certificate-bank statistics and the 2026-09-28 timing/projection worksheet, none of which is named anywhere in the paper; the Appendix A figures trace to ledger rows and commits. Either name those receipts (by file name) in the Artifacts or Data-availability paragraph, or narrow the sentence to "Every census count".
- **§4.6 [L204–208] — projection paragraph relies on rates the paper does not source.** "5.5 GB per Kissat solve-hour", "about half a megabyte of gzipped certificate per second", and "eight to a host" are the load points of the whole cube-route projection; the first two now have a receipt (`CERT_BANK_STATS_20260926.md`, `CENSUS_TIMING_20260928.md` in `refs/`) but the body gives the reader no pointer to where they were measured (12,102 bank rows binned by solve time; the replay pilot). One sentence naming the bank sample size and the pilot would let `paper-audit` and a reader check the projection.
- **§4.2 H1 [L165] — 1,161 residual roots vs 1,158 gap slots.** The paper states both numbers correctly (they match `CENSUS.md`) but never says why three residual roots are not among the 1,288 capacity-grid gaps. A careful reader will stop here. One clause ("three residual roots carry a certificate object in the capacity grid and so are not gap slots", or whatever the receipt says) is needed; do not guess the reason — take it from the census receipts.
- **§3.2 [L105] — the strata are still not defined.** "the normalization that assigns a candidate graph to a stratum and the exact meaning of ``high'' are fixed by the Lean definitions and are not restated here." The reviser correctly refused to invent a definition absent from `refs/`, but the gap is now the paper's largest rigor hole: a reader cannot see that h ∈ {1,3,5,7} exhausts the candidates (why no even h? what is a "high" vertex — degree ≥ 8?). Fix on the authors' side: add a two-sentence statement of the normalization to `refs/` (from the Lean source) so the next revision can state it; until then the paper should at least say what quantity h counts.
- **§2 Related work — positioning is accurate but thin.** `related-work` The section never says by what methods the decided entries of Boza's table (or the earlier exact values of Zhang–Chen–Cheng) were obtained, so a reader cannot tell whether a two-solver SAT census is a new instrument for this function or the same instrument at larger scale; nor does it situate the census among prior SAT-based extremal/Ramsey computations beyond the two Heule et al. citations. A `paper-litsearch` pass should cover (i) the methods behind the existing exact values of $r(s)=R(C_4,K_{1,s})$ and of $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)$ at comparable orders, and (ii) prior SAT-with-certificate results in extremal combinatorics. No specific citations are proposed here (web search off; nothing is to be invented).
- **§4.2 H3/H5 and H7 [L139–141] — the three non-H1 strata are summaries of receipts the reader cannot see.** "two checked cover formulas and a $7\times 8$ grid of positive cubes per cell", "43 classes; a universal singleton-capacity argument excludes 15" — the shapes of the arguments are named but not sketched. For D2 to rise, give one paragraph each on what a "cover formula" and a "singleton-capacity argument" are, or move the H7 closure ledger (`refs/H7_CLOSURE_20260915.md`) into an appendix table with the 28 roots and their review chains.

## minor

- **§1 Significance [L58] and Abstract [L39] — "the first strict drop of $f$ decided".** Within the BRIEF's tolerance (the phrase is epistemic and conditional on the evidence level), but "decided" is the verb the literature check's guardrail 2 reserves for the certificate-audited case, and it sits one word from the BRIEF's hard rule. Consider "the first strict drop of $f$ settled at the level of computational evidence" or "established at this evidence level", which keeps the priority claim without the reserved verb.
- **§4.6 [L204] — "two to four weeks of fleet time depending on shard count".** The fleet-cost JSON's byte-weighted planned wall days are 31.5 / 15.7 / 7.9 for 8 / 16 / 32 shards, i.e. about one to four and a half weeks. Quote the range from the receipt or say "one to four and a half weeks (8 to 32 planned days at 32 to 8 shards)".
- **§4.6 [L204] — "roughly 65 to 130 GB".** The worksheet prints 60–130 GB (12–24 h × 5.5 GB/h); the arithmetic gives 66–132, so 65 is defensible but does not match the receipt's printed range. Align with the receipt or state the arithmetic.

- **§4.1 [L135] — non-isomorphism claim.** The script is named in §7 but no receipt (output, hash) is cited. Name the receipt or say "reproducible from the archived script".
- **§4.2 Table 3 [L158] — Cap cell "probe 1 h, leaves 24 h" wraps to a lone "h".** Widen the Cap column (0.17 → 0.20) or write "1 h probe / 24 h leaves".
- **§5.1 [L222] — campaign jargon.** "cold-green", "jaw", "socket", "monoliths", "pincers" are room vocabulary. Define each at first use or replace with plain terms (an outside reader does not know that "cold-green" means "elaborates in a cold build").
