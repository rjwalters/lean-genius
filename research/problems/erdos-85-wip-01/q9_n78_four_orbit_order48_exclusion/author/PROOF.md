# No order-48 action with four orbits of sizes 6,24,24,24

Assume a simple C4-free nine-regular graph G on 78 vertices has automorphism group A of order 48, with four vertex orbits F,U,V,R of sizes 6,24,24,24. Assume the necessary quotient form submitted as 2314: F is a matching of three edges; every U or V vertex has one F neighbor; each F center has four U and four V neighbors; and R has no F neighbors. We exclude this structure directly. The corollary that a four-orbit graph must have |A|=24 is conditional on acceptance of the 2314 quotient reduction.

## Local involution bounds

The accepted premises summarized and proved in 2257 imply every nonidentity involution fixes at most six vertices. At any fixed vertex it has either one or three fixed neighbors. If it fixes exactly six vertices, their induced graph is either 3K2 or a triangle with a pendant leaf at each triangle vertex. These fixed-graph classifications are independent of whether the involution belongs to the action kernel on F.

Choose f in F and its matching partner f'. They have the same point stabilizer H: any automorphism fixing one fixes its unique F matching partner, and conversely. Its order is 48/6=8. Put U_f=N(f) intersect U and V_f=N(f) intersect V, and similarly at f'. Each set has four vertices. H is transitive on each: given two vertices in, for example, U_f, transitivity of A on U gives an automorphism mapping one to the other; their unique F neighbors force that automorphism to fix f. Hence each four-point H action has a point stabilizer of order two.

Every involution of H fixes f' and therefore fixes either zero or two of the other eight neighbors U_f union V_f, by the local involution bound. The same assertion holds at f'. In particular no involution can fix four points in either fiber, nor two in each of the two fibers.

## The two reflection classes must both occur

If H were abelian, the nonidentity element of a four-point fiber stabilizer would fix the entire fiber, contradicting the preceding bound. If H were quaternion of order eight, its unique involution is central and the same contradiction follows for every order-two point stabilizer. The elementary classification of groups of order eight (audited in 2298) therefore leaves H=D8.

Write H=<r,s | r^4=s^2=1, srs=r^-1>. Its involutions are the central r^2 and the four reflections, in conjugacy classes {s,r^2 s} and {rs,r^3 s}. A point stabilizer of either four-point fiber cannot be <r^2>, because centrality would make r^2 fix the whole fiber. It is generated by a reflection.

For a transitive action on four points with stabilizer <t>, the fixed-point count for a reflection q is |C_H(q)|/2=2 if q is conjugate to t, and zero otherwise. This is the standard coset count: a fixed coset representative conjugates q to the unique nonidentity element t, giving |C_H(q)| representatives when possible, and two representatives per coset. In D8 a reflection centralizer has four elements.

The U_f and V_f stabilizer reflections cannot belong to the same conjugacy class: a reflection in that class would fix two points in each fiber, contrary to the zero-or-two bound. They therefore use the two distinct reflection classes. Identically, the two fibers U_f',V_f' use the two distinct classes of the same group H.

## Contradiction in the fixed graph

Take any reflection q in H. It fixes exactly two vertices in U_f union V_f, and exactly two in U_f' union V_f'. These two neighborhoods are disjoint, since every attached vertex has a unique F neighbor. Together with f and f' there are at least six fixed vertices; the upper bound six makes these exactly the fixed set. Both f and f' have three fixed neighbors: each other and its two fixed attached neighbors. Thus they are adjacent cubic vertices in the fixed graph.

Their edge has no common G-neighbor. A common neighbor cannot lie in F because F is a matching; cannot lie in U or V because those vertices have unique F neighbors; and cannot lie in R because R has no F neighbors. The fixed graph is not 3K2 because f and f' have degree three. It cannot be a triangle with three pendant leaves either: every edge between its two cubic endpoints belongs to the triangle and has a common neighbor. This contradiction excludes the assumed action.

Consequently, once the 2314 reduction is accepted, a four-orbit candidate can only have automorphism order 24 and orbit sizes 6,24,24,24, with the two remaining quotient alternatives from that reduction. This proof does not exclude order24, five or more orbits, all N78 graphs, N80, or Erdős85. It is a paper argument without enumeration, graph solver, or Lean formalization.
