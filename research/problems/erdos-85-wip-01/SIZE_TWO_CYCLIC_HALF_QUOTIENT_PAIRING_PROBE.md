# Half-quotient parity pairing probe

Date: 2026-08-24

Owner: codex-sol-1

Scope: `BinarySizeTwoCyclicPackingBound`; divergence-round #7 binary descent

## Verdict

Reducing a `q=2m` routing block modulo `m` and then modulo two produces an
exact four-point boundary: two consecutive odd row vertices and two
consecutive odd column vertices.  This is a useful concrete form of the
first augmentation quotient.

The proposed three-valued path-pairing invariant is **not canonical**.  The
projected block is an arbitrary binary bipartite 1-chain with the prescribed
boundary, and two such chains differ by the full cycle space of
`K_(m,m)`.  The three visual ways of pairing four boundary points differ by
cycles and cannot be read from the chain without an extra planar embedding
or a chosen path decomposition.  Neither is present in the reciprocal code.

Thus first-level `q -> q/2` parity descent alone collapses exactly to the
known consecutive-hole boundary.  A viable descent must retain the odd-lift
cocycle (the next augmentation coefficient), not merely the projected
support and its four odd vertices.

## Exact projection

Fix `(x,t)` and use relative absolute coordinates for its partial
permutation:

```text
row coordinate     r = y-x,
column coordinate  p = z-x.
```

There is one edge in every row except `t,t+1`, and one edge in every column
except `0,-1`.  Let `pi : Z/q -> Z/m` be reduction, and define the binary
matrix

```text
B_(x,t)(i,j)
  = #{ routing edges with pi(r)=i and pi(p)=j } mod 2.
```

Every residue modulo `m` has two lifts.  A row residue different from
`pi(t),pi(t+1)` retains both lifts and therefore has even projected degree.
Each of the two hole residues loses one lift and has odd degree.  Since
consecutive residues remain distinct when `m>1`,

```text
rowBoundary(B_(x,t)) = e_(pi t) + e_(pi(t+1)).
```

The identical column calculation gives

```text
columnBoundary(B_(x,t)) = e_0 + e_(-1).
```

This uses only the exact two-punctured permutation law.  It is independent
of the locations of the surviving entries and agrees with the valuation-one
calculation in `SIZE_TWO_CYCLIC_AUGMENTATION_FILTRATION_PROBE.md`.

## Why there is no pairing type

Regard `B` as a 1-chain in the complete bipartite graph with row shore
`R=Z/m` and column shore `C=Z/m`.  Its graph boundary is precisely the four
vertices displayed above.  It is tempting to decompose the chain into paths
and record one of the three pairings of those vertices.

Path decomposition is not unique.  More algebraically, if `B` and `B'` have
the same row and column boundary, then `D=B+B'` has zero degree at every
vertex and hence lies in the binary cycle space of `K_(m,m)`.  Conversely
every such cycle preserves the boundary.  This cycle space is generated by
the four-cycles

```text
(i,j) + (i,j0) + (i0,j) + (i0,j0)
```

and has dimension `(m-1)^2`.  Adding a suitable four-cycle changes how a
chosen path decomposition pairs the boundary endpoints.  Therefore the
boundary quotient has one affine class, not three intrinsic pairing
classes.

The code's C4-free hypothesis does not remove these algebraic four-cycles:
the four entries above live inside one **projected parity block** and may
come from different lifts.  A four-cycle after quotienting modulo `m` is not
a four-cycle of the original exterior graph.  Treating it as forbidden
would silently strengthen the hypotheses.

## Reciprocity does not repair the loss at first level

For an original route

```text
(x,t) --r--> (y,s),       y=x+r,
```

the point in the absolute partial-permutation block is `(y,z)` with
`z=y+s`.  Reversal puts the point `(x,x+t)` in the block indexed by `(y,s)`.
Thus reciprocity couples individual lifted entries of different blocks.  It
does not choose a path decomposition of either projected chain, and adding
projected cycles changes no four-point boundary for it to detect.

Consequently a global argument based only on the matrices `B_(x,t)` and
their row/column boundary vectors sees no more than the already-known holes.
The potentially nontrivial datum is the lift bit of each edge over its
modulo-`m` projection.  Reciprocity negates the original displacement and
therefore acts on those lift bits by an affine cocycle.  Deriving an exact
law for that cocycle is the next legitimate descent question.

## Stop condition

Do not formalize a `three pairing types` theorem: it is false without an
extra choice, and any chosen type is not invariant under the projected cycle
space.  Reopen binary descent only with a statement that retains one of:

1. the lift bit / second augmentation coefficient of every routed dart;
2. an original-graph condition that forbids the relevant lifted cycles;
3. an exact reciprocity equation on the global collection of lift cocycles.

Merely iterating the four-odd-vertex boundary from `q` to `q/2` adds no
coupling and is another one-block hill climb.
