# H3 Python/native input correspondence

The two frozen H3 inputs are Python-generated broad canonical bases, not literal byte-identical native Lean scout inputs. This audit identifies their relationship without changing either input or launching a solver.

For profile 0, the bijection from Python lazy edge allocation to Lean lexicographic IDs (1176 variables, auxiliary IDs fixed) maps every one of 1,328,183 clauses exactly to native `h3_b1.cnf` after removing its 84 geometry clauses immediately after the 141 fixed units. Both files have 29,500 variables. There is no residual clause difference or trailing data.

For profile 1, the same edge-ID bijection matches 1,328,083 of 1,328,183 clauses of native `h3_dist2.cnf` after its 108 geometry clauses are removed. The 100 differences are support/partition clauses. A low-vertex permutation, fixing all high vertices and listed in `support-results.json`, matches the complete fixed-unit and partition-clause multisets. Both profiles have the identical 1,327,904-clause C4/degree block before the low-vertex permutation. Profile 0 has support multiplicities 1 each for the three high pairs, 6 each for the three singletons, and 25 empty; profile 1 has one high triple, 7 each singleton, and 24 empty.

The semantic argument is that C4-freeness and the degree target (8 at high vertices, 7 at low vertices) are invariant under low-vertex relabeling. The matched degree-counter block encodes those exact degrees, with auxiliary extensions supplied by the existing counter correctness theory. Auxiliary clauses are NOT claimed byte-identical after vertex relabeling. This is a computational comparison plus a mathematical correspondence argument, not a new Lean equivalence theorem. In particular, UNSAT of the broad base implies UNSAT of its corresponding specialized scout; the reverse implication is not claimed.

This audit does not by itself prove the exhaustive coverage of all scout geometries or the full H3 stratum. It does not upgrade these Python bytes to literal native bytes.
