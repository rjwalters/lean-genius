# Every two-subgroup at N78 has order at most32

Let G be a simple C4-free graph on78 vertices with minimum degree nine, and let P be a subgroup of its automorphism group whose order is a power of two. Then |P|<=32. In fact any two-subgroup H fixing a vertex has order at most16.

## Accepted graph premises

Near-Moore regularity gives degree nine. Accepted2220,2236,2237 imply that every nonidentity involution has at most six fixed vertices. For an involution fixing a vertex v, the number of fixed neighbors of v is odd: its other neighbors are paired. If there are at most four fixed vertices, this odd number is at most three. If there are six, accepted2238/2240 leave only the fixed graph3K2 or a triangle with one pendant leaf per centre, also of maximum degree three. Consequently every nonidentity involution fixing v fixes either one or three neighbors of v.

## Vertex stabilizer bound

Let H be a two-subgroup fixing v. Its action on the nine neighbors of v has a fixed point x, since all nontrivial H orbits have even size. Therefore H acts on the remaining eight neighbors Omega=N(v) minus{x}. This action is faithful. Indeed a nontrivial kernel would be a nontrivial finite two-group and would contain an involution. That involution would fix v and all nine neighbors, contrary to the six-fixed-vertex bound.

Every nonidentity involution in H fixes zero or two points of Omega: it fixes x, and its total number of fixed neighbors is one or three. Thus it moves at least six points of Omega.

If H is trivial there is nothing to prove. Otherwise its centre contains an involution z, and H is contained in the permutation centralizer of z on Omega. There are two cases.

(a) z has no fixed points on Omega, so it is a product of four disjoint transpositions. Its centralizer permutes those four pairs. The kernel B of this pair action consists of independent swaps within the pairs, so B is elementary abelian of order16. The quotient is isomorphic to S4. Put K=H intersect B. Any nonidentity element of K swaps at least three of the four pairs, because it is an involution moving at least six points. As a binary linear code of length four, K therefore has all nonzero words of weight at least three.

Such a code has dimension at most one: two distinct words of weight three differ in exactly two positions; a word of weight four and one of weight three differ in one position. Thus two distinct nonzero permitted words have a forbidden nonzero sum. It follows that |K|<=2. The image of H in S4 is a two-group, of order at most eight. Hence |H|<=2*8=16.

(b) z fixes two points of Omega and swaps the other six in three pairs. Its centralizer permutes the three nontrivial pairs and also preserves the two-point fixed set. Map this centralizer to S3 by its action on the three pairs. The kernel B consists of three independent within-pair swaps and the optional swap of the two fixed points. It is again elementary abelian of order16, generated by four disjoint transpositions on Omega. The identical minimum-weight argument gives |H intersect B|<=2. The image in S3 is a two-group, of order at most two. Thus |H|<=4 in this case.

This proves the vertex stabilizer bound16 without any classification of two-groups.

## Whole two-subgroup bound

All P orbit sizes on78 vertices are powers of two. Since78 is not divisible by four, there must be an orbit of size one or two; otherwise all orbit sizes would be divisible by four. Choose v in such an orbit. The subgroup P_v fixes v, so |P_v|<=16 by the preceding argument. Orbit-stabilizer gives |P|=|P.v|*|P_v|<=2*16=32.

This bounds orders of arbitrary two-subgroups, including Sylow two-subgroups; it is stronger in scope than the separate2253 element-order bound, which is not used in this proof. It does not exclude groups of order32, settle the remaining symmetry cases, or solve the N78 graph problem. This is a paper proof, not a Lean formalization or graph search.
