# Exclusion of the order-two kernel in the N78 three-orbit case

Assume the N78 three-orbit action of2264/2268 and the K2 image restriction submitted as2306. The latter gives central K={1,z}, B=A/K isomorphic to rotational S4 on six oriented axes, cyclic C4 center stabilizers in B, and center stabilizers H in A of type C8 or C4 times C2. The following proof excludes both possibilities. Its conclusion remains conditional until2306 and this packet receive independent acceptance.

## Cyclic center stabilizers are impossible

The W48 action is regular under A. Choosing an origin gives an inverse-closed Cayley connection set S of size five. Each of the six attached groups has size eight. C4-freeness permits at most one W-neighbor in the same attached group and each of the four other groups whose fixed centers are not matched to its center; the matched group is forbidden. Thus W-degree five saturates those five slots.

In particular S intersects the center stabilizer H in exactly one element. This element is a nonidentity involution because S and H are inverse-closed. If H=C8 it must be z, its unique involution, since K is its order-two subgroup. But z is central in A. For any other s in S, the four distinct W vertices 1,z,zs,s form a C4 using right generators z and s (and their inverses). Hence H cannot be cyclic. All center stabilizers therefore have type C4 times C2 and exponent four.

## A splits as S4 times C2

Under the cube-diagonal identification in2306, the three nonidentity even coordinate flips act as double transpositions on the four diagonal pairs. They are the involutions that fix oriented axes. The six remaining involutions, which fix no oriented axis, act as the six transpositions in S4. Therefore the residual stabilizer involution ell projects to a transposition. By conjugating ell in A, every S4 transposition has an involutive lift.

Choose such lifts a,b,c of the adjacent transpositions (12),(23),(34). Their squares are identity. Each of (ab)^3 and (bc)^3 belongs to central K. Multiply a or c by z as necessary so both cubes are identity; these changes preserve all three squares. The disjoint images of a,c commute, so (ac)^2 lies in K. Write (ac)^2=z^e, with e in{0,1}; equivalently ca=z^e ac.

The braid relations aba=bab and bcb=cbc give

    (abc)^2 = z^e abacbc = z^e babbcb = z^e bacb,
    (abc)^4 = b(ac)^2 b = z^e.

The image of abc is a four-cycle. Every cyclic order-four subgroup in S4 is conjugate to a point stabilizer of its oriented-axis action, so this image fixes a center. Its lift abc fixes the same center, hence belongs to a center stabilizer of exponent four. Thus (abc)^4=1 and e=0. We have ac=ca.

The subgroup G0 generated by a,b,c has order at most24. Here is an elementary normal-form verification, avoiding a central-extension classification. The subgroup U generated by a,b has order six, from a^2=b^2=(ab)^3=1 and its S3 image. The four representatives 1,c,cb,cba suffice for left U cosets. Right multiplication by a,b,c rewrites as follows (a or b preceding a representative belongs to U):

    1:   a,       b,       c
    c:   a*c,     cb,      1
    cb:  cba,     c,       b*cb
    cba: cb,      a*cba,   b*cba.

The relations above verify every entry. Therefore the union of those four U cosets is closed under the generators and contains G0, giving |G0|<=24. Its image in S4 is all of S4, so |G0|=24 and G0 intersects K trivially. Since K is central and A is generated by G0 and K,

    A = G0 times K, isomorphic to S4 times C2.

Moreover K is its central factor, and H is the full inverse image C4 times C2 of a cyclic order-four subgroup of S4.
