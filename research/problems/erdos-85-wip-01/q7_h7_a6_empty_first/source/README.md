# H7 a6 empty-first incidence normal form

Assume the established H7 premises and accepted2091. At a6, three highs have singleton-empty incidence sum3 and four have sum4. Let R be any simple graph on the seven high colours with degree sequence2^3,3^4, and let Q=K7 minusR. Then Q has twelve edges. Let F be the empty-induced graph: seven vertices, six edges, maximum degree3, no C4.

As in accepted2097, the pair-empty incidence is a proper edge-colouring phi:E(Q)->V(F) with colour class x of size deg_F(x). Pij has that unique empty host when ij is in Q and none otherwise. At high i let U_i be the unused empty colours. Its size is3 at each of the three sum3 highs and4 at the four sum4 highs.

For |U_i|=3, choose one pair for the double-empty singleton and the remaining singleton host for the single-empty singleton: three choices. For |U_i|=4, partition U_i into two unordered pairs, hosting the two double-empty singletons: again three choices. Number the pair containing min(U_i) first as a canonical convention. The two singletons have identical support and empty degree, so swapping their names loses no graph; future remaining-low edges may be relabelled with them. There are exactly3^7=2,187 canonical singleton-incidence choices for each fixed F,phi.

There are eleven double-empty singleton host pairs and three single-empty hosts. Every high has degree8. At empty vertex x, its deg_F(x) incident pair vertices cover2deg_F(x) distinct high colours; every other high contributes exactly one singleton. Its total degree is therefore deg_F(x)+deg_F(x)+(7-2deg_F(x))=7. Every high-empty pair has exactly one common neighbour.

The resulting high/empty-complete partial49vertex graph is C4-free exactly when all eleven double-host pairs are distinct and each pair has no common F-neighbour. The vertex-pair proof of2097 extends unchanged: all same-high singleton host sets are disjoint, including the two double-host sets at a sum4 high; pair/singleton high overlap excludes simultaneous empty overlap by unused-colour construction. Two different singleton vertices can only create a second common neighbour through a repeated double-host pair. Empty/nonempty-low and empty/empty common-neighbour constraints reduce to absence of a common F-neighbour for a selected pair and absence of duplicate selected pairs. The other pair types are already controlled by F C4-freeness and proper phi.

For fixed F,phi this is a seven-row disjoint-pair-packing problem: each row has at most three choices; a sum3 row selects one pair value and a sum4 row selects two disjoint pair values. All selected values across rows must be distinct and individually F-admissible. This is not an ordinary single-representative matching problem because four rows consume two pair values.
