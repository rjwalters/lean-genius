# An order-eight element cannot lie in a larger abelian two-subgroup

Let G be a simple C4-free graph on78 vertices with minimum degree9. Use the accepted bound from2220,2236,2237: every nonidentity involution fixes an even number of vertices at most6. Let A be an abelian subgroup of Aut(G) of power-of-two order, containing an element x of order8. We prove A is exactly the cyclic subgroup generated by x.

Consider an A-orbit O. If its point stabilizer H is nontrivial, H contains a nonidentity involution t. Since A is abelian, t fixes every point of O: if t fixes v, then t(av)=a(tv)=av for every a in A. Consequently |O|<=6. Orbit sizes are powers of two, so every nonfree orbit has size1,2,or4. Every free orbit has size|A|.

Put z=x^4, a nonidentity involution. On a nonfree orbit, the action factors through A/H, whose order is|O|<=4. Every element of that quotient has order dividing4, so x^4 lies in H and fixes the orbit. On a free orbit, z fixes no point. Thus Fix(z) is exactly the union of the nonfree orbits. If f=|Fix(z)|, we obtain

    |A| divides 78-f, with f in{0,2,4,6}.

Since A contains x, its order is at least8. Of the four possible moved cardinalities78,76,74,72, only72 is divisible by8, and none is divisible by16. Hence f=6 and |A|=8. Therefore A=<x>.

In particular Aut(G) has no subgroup C8 times C2. More generally, every automorphism y of power-of-two order commuting with x belongs to <x>: the subgroup generated by the commuting elements x,y is an abelian two-group, so the theorem applies. Thus x^4 is the unique nonidentity involution that can commute with x. This does not exclude nonabelian two-subgroups containing x, nor does it bound the order of the whole automorphism group by8.

The proof uses only the accepted involution fixed-count bound and elementary finite group actions. It is not a graph search, a Lean formalization, or a full N78 graph exclusion.
