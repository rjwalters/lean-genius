# Exclusion of every three-two-orbit S-action at order16

Assume the N78 simple C4-free9-regular graph has full automorphism group A of order16, with the matching locus S split into three two-point A-orbits. The separately submitted universal geometry2445 is an explicit independent-review dependency. It supplies W=W0 union W1 union W2, three regular16-orbits, and R=R0 union R1 union R2, three transitive eight-point orbits. R is cubic, each point stabilizer on R has order two, and every W vertex has exactly one neighbor in each R_i. No size of the kernel on S is assumed here.

## Stabilizer classes and cross-pair capacity

Every involution fixing an R vertex is noncentral: if central it fixes that entire eight-point orbit, contrary to the six-fixed bound2257. Accepted2438 gives a central involution z fixing S; any noncentral stabilizer involution t is distinct from z. Its centralizer contains the four-element group generated by z and t. Thus the conjugacy class of t has size at most four, and as a noncentral element in a2-group its class has size two or four.

Label each R_i by the conjugacy class of its stabilizer involution. For a class of size c, the fixed-point count on any R_i of that class is |C_A(t)|/2=8/c. An involution from a different class fixes no vertex there. These are the standard coset-action counts: g^-1 t g must be the unique nonidentity element of the order-two point stabilizer.

For i distinct from j, the64 ordered pairs in R_i times R_j have nontrivial stabilizer precisely when their two endpoint stabilizers coincide. If the two orbit classes differ, all64 pairs are free. If they agree and have size c, each of the eight first endpoints has8/c partners with the same stabilizer, giving64/c nonfree pairs and64-64/c free pairs.

Each W_k maps equivariantly to R_i times R_j by its two unique neighbors. This map is injective because a pair cannot have two common neighbors in a C4-free graph. Its image is16 free pairs, and the three W_k images are disjoint. Hence at least48 free pairs are necessary for every pair of residual orbits. In particular two residual orbits cannot have the same size-two stabilizer class, which would leave only32 free pairs. When they have the same size-four class, there are exactly48 free pairs and the W images exhaust all of them.

In that saturated case any R middle with neighbors in both of these residual orbits has endpoint neighbors with equal stabilizers. Otherwise this free endpoint pair would already have a W common neighbor, creating a C4 with the R middle.

## Orbit degrees

Write d_ij for the constant number of R_j-neighbors of a vertex in R_i. The matrix d is symmetric by equal orbit sizes and edge balance; all row sums are three. Internal degree three is impossible by the cubic-eight lemma2425. If R_i and R_j have different stabilizer classes, d_ij is even: an order-two stabilizer at a vertex of R_i acts without fixed points on R_j and hence partitions its invariant neighbor set into pairs.

We distinguish all possible equality patterns of the three stabilizer classes.

### Three distinct classes

All off-diagonal degrees are even. Each diagonal degree is consequently odd and cannot be three, so it is one. The off-diagonal row sum is two; each vertex of the three-orbit degree diagram must therefore have exactly one off-diagonal entry equal to two. Symmetry would give a perfect matching on three indices, impossible.

### Exactly two equal classes

Relabel so R0,R1 have the same class and R2 has a different class. The repeated class must have size four by the capacity obstruction above. The two off-diagonal entries in row2 are even, so d22=1 and precisely one equals two. Relabel the repeated pair so d20=d02=2 and d21=d12=0.

Set x=d01=d10. Row sums give d00=1-x and d11=3-x. Nonnegativity gives x<=1. The value x=0 would give d11=3, already excluded. Thus x=1, d00=0 and d11=2.

Fix r in R1. Its sole R0-neighbor u is fixed by A_r, so A_u=A_r since both stabilizers have order two. Each of its two internal R1-neighbors v forms, with u, a cross R0/R1 pair having common neighbor r. Saturation forces A_v=A_u=A_r. But the involution generating A_r fixes exactly two points of R1: one is r, so only one other vertex of R1 can have this same stabilizer. There cannot be two distinct internal neighbors of that type. Contradiction.

### All three classes equal

The common class must again have size four. Every cross pair of residual orbits is saturated. No degree-matrix row can be supported at only one index: a diagonal3 is excluded, and an off-diagonal3 saturates both corresponding rows, forcing the third diagonal to be3. Therefore every cubic residual neighborhood meets at least two residual orbits, with a singleton intersection in one of them.

For r choose its unique neighbor u in such an orbit. Then A_u=A_r. Each other neighbor lies in a different orbit from u, so saturation forces its stabilizer also to equal A_r. Thus all R-edges preserve the point stabilizer. For each of the four possible stabilizer involutions there are exactly two fixed points in each R_i, totaling six vertices. Every R-edge stays in one such set, and each induced six-point graph is cubic. But in a simple C4-free cubic graph there are six distinct nonreturning two-step endpoints from every vertex, impossible with only five other vertices. Contradiction.

These three cases exhaust all stabilizer-class equality patterns. Hence the three-two-orbit S-action is impossible for every kernel size, conditional on2445's universal geometry. Together with2419's original alternatives, any surviving full order16 action must have S-orbits2+4. This conclusion is a structural restriction, not exclusion of all order16 groups or graph existence. No finite enumeration, capped-domain retry, or Lean formalization is used. Independent review is requested.
