# Every full order-sixteen action has the regular-eight matching normal form

Let G be simple, C4-free and nine-regular on 78 vertices, with full automorphism group A of order 16. Accepted 2315 bounds every vertex stabilizer by 8. Accepted 2412 supplies a normal subgroup H of order 8, the stabilizer of an A-orbit of size two. Its common fixed set F is a matching on 2, 4 or 6 vertices. Every A-orbit in F has size two. At each f in F, H acts on its neighbors with orbit sizes 1+8 or 1+4+4, with the single common fixed neighbor its matching partner.

Call the latter local action split. We show that no vertex in F has a split action. The property of being split is A-invariant because H is normal.

In a split action both transitive four-point actions are faithful by 2412, so H is D8. Each point stabilizer in a faithful four-point action has order two and cannot be the central subgroup (which would be a kernel). Thus it is generated by a reflection. D8 has two conjugacy classes of reflection subgroups. The two local four-point actions must use different classes: equal classes give equivalent coset actions and a reflection fixing four of those eight points, contrary to 2257. A reflection fixes two points in the action of its own class and zero in the other. Consequently every reflection of H fixes exactly two neighbors outside F at every split vertex.

If |F|=4 and a split vertex exists, its A-orbit gives two distinct split vertices f,g. Choose any reflection h of H. It fixes all four vertices of F and two outside neighbors at each of f,g. C4-freeness says f and g have at most one common neighbor, so these outside sets have union of size at least three. This gives at least seven fixed vertices of h, contrary to 2257.

If |F|=2, write F={v,x}, an edge. The A-action exchanges them, so if either is split both are. They have no common neighbor: any such neighbor is unique by C4-freeness, hence fixed by H, but F has no third vertex. A reflection h therefore fixes the six distinct vertices v,x and two additional neighbors at each. The six-fixed-vertex bound says these are its entire fixed set. Both v and x have degree three in the induced fixed graph, are adjacent, and have no common neighbor. This is neither 3K2 nor a triangle with one pendant leaf at each triangle vertex: adjacent degree-three vertices in the latter share the third triangle vertex. These are the only allowed six-fixed graphs, as recorded in the accepted graph premises of 2257. Contradiction.

If |F|=6, accepted 2412 shows that H acts freely outside F. Thus the eight outside neighbors of every f form a regular H-orbit, and no split vertex exists.

We conclude that every f in F has a locally regular eight-point action. Choose any matched pair v,x in F. Both local regularity hypotheses of accepted 2263 hold. Accepted 2263 and 2269 then give the following necessary structure:

* The set S of vertices with nontrivial H-stabilizer has size six and induces 3K2. H acts freely on its complement.
* W consists of the 48 vertices with exactly one S-neighbor; R consists of the other 24 vertices, with no S-neighbor.
* The equitable quotient, ordered S,W,R, is [[1,8,0],[1,5,3],[0,6,3]]. In particular R is cubic.
* For E=8I+J-M^2, E[S]=K6, E(R,W) is empty, and E[R] is five-regular.

Normality of H makes S A-invariant, and then W and R are A-invariant by their definitions. S has no A-fixed vertex, so its A-orbit sizes are either 2+4 or 2+2+2. For a vertex outside S, its A-stabilizer intersects H trivially and injects into A/H. Its order is at most two, so outside A-orbits have size 8 or 16. Therefore R has orbit sizes 8+16 or 8+8+8; W has a decomposition into sizes 8 and 16 summing to 48.

The distinction between F=Fix(H) and S, the nonfree locus of H, is essential: this proof does not assert F=S or |F|=2. This is a necessary normal form for full order 16, not an exclusion of order 16 or of graph existence. No finite graph search or Lean formalization is claimed.
