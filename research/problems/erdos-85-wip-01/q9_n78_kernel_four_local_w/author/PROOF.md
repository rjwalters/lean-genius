Fix a W vertex w. It has one S-neighbor s, three R-neighbors, and five W-neighbors. Every residual vertex has three residual neighbors. The nine two-step endpoints obtained from w through its three R-neighbors are distinct by C4-freeness. Their set C has size nine. For every residual r, E(w,r)=0 by2419, hence w and r have exactly one common neighbor. There are no S-to-R edges. Therefore the remaining fifteen residual vertices R minus C must be reached exactly once through the five W-neighbors of w. Each candidate W-neighbor v has its already known three R-neighbors, so the five residual triples must be pairwise disjoint and partition R minus C.

There is a second independent requirement. E[S]=K6 in the defect graph of degree five exhausts the defect neighbors of each S vertex. Thus w has exactly one common neighbor with each S vertex. Its S-neighbor s supplies the length-two endpoint s*, the matching partner of s. Its R-neighbors supply no S endpoints. Consequently its five W-neighbors must have five distinct S centers, namely all S vertices except s*. A vertex cannot be its own neighbor, so w itself is removed from candidate sets.

These statements give an exact necessary local set-cover instance: for each of the five permitted centers, choose one W vertex with residual triple contained in R minus C; the five triples must partition that set. All candidate sets are explicitly saved. A memoized recursion processes these five sets and subtracts a triple only if contained in the remaining endpoint mask. Every option is tried on a negative result. A local positive would provide one witness only, not an exhaustive list of potential neighborhoods and not a simultaneous graph completion.

It suffices to test one origin from each of the three regular W orbits, since the input incidences and S matching are A-invariant. The checker nevertheless tests all three origins in every case.
