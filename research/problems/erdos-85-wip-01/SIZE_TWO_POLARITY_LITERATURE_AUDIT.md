# Size-two polarity literature audit

Node: `A-REG-NONBIP / NONBIP-MIXED`, all normalized component weights two.

Status: outside-first audit after the direct support-rank lane plateaued.  This
identifies the exact classical absolute-point theorem nearest to the required
branch-wide terminal, and the hypothesis our defect components fail.

## The classical extension beyond projective planes

Hoffman, Newman, Straus and Taussky, *On the number of absolute points of a
correlation*, Pacific Journal of Mathematics **6** (1956), 83--96,
DOI `10.2140/pjm.1956.6.83`, develops the incidence-matrix proof of the
Baer--Ball absolute-point results.  Section 7 explicitly extends the method
from projective planes to symmetric group-divisible designs.

In their notation a symmetric group-divisible design has incidence matrix
`N`, block-diagonal group matrix `K`, and constants `lambda_1, lambda_2` such
that

```text
N N^T = (k-lambda_1) I
      + (lambda_1-lambda_2) K
      + lambda_2 J.                                  (HNST)
```

A correlation is represented by a permutation matrix `P` with
`N^T = P N`.  The four normal matrices occurring in the resulting identity
commute and can be simultaneously diagonalized.  The authors then factor the
characteristic polynomial into cyclotomic square-root factors; the trace of
the correlation incidence matrix counts its absolute points.

This is precisely the classical matrix mechanism one would want here: a
zero-diagonal symmetric incidence labeling would be a polarity with no
absolute points, and a suitable trace restriction would rule it out.

## Why it does not apply to the A-REG blocks

For the binary square-order problem the ambient adjacency/incidence matrix
satisfies

```text
A A^T = (q-1) I + J - D,
```

where the groups are the connected components of the defect graph `D`.
Between distinct groups every pair has intersection number one.  Inside one
group, however, the intersection number is

```text
0  on a D-edge,
1  on a D-nonedge.
```

Thus a nontrivial connected defect component has two variable within-group
intersection relations, not the single constant `lambda_1` required by
`(HNST)`.  Replacing `D` by the coarse group matrix loses exactly the
connected nonbipartite information that the terminal must use.  The binary
biaffine polarity calculation is a constant-intersection special case; it
cannot be promoted to arbitrary selectors merely by citing the
group-divisible theorem.

Equivalently, the HNST proof needs the group matrix and the correlation
permutation to live in one commuting normal algebra.  In the present problem
the extra matrix is the full arbitrary regular graph `D`, and symmetry of
`A` gives `AD=DA` but does not put `D` in the two-class algebra generated by
`I,J,K`.  Its nonprincipal spectrum and eigenspace multiplicities survive.

## Exact missing bridge

A branch-wide absolute-point consumer would therefore need one of the
following genuinely stronger inputs.

1. An association-scheme refinement showing that every relevant component
   adjacency `D[C]` lies in a correlation-invariant commutative adjacency
   algebra whose primitive idempotents have controlled multiplicities.
2. A Hoffman--Newman--Straus--Taussky trace theorem allowing an arbitrary
   regular within-group relation `D`, using only
   `A^2=(q-1)I+J-D`, `AD=DA`, integrality, and the reciprocal component
   placement.
3. A structural theorem forcing constant within-group intersections (or the
   affine minimal-trade normal form).  This would close only the resulting
   restricted subclass unless the forcing theorem itself is branch-wide.

The first two formulations expose the same already-known arithmetic danger:
on every `D`-eigenspace the roots of `A` are
`+-sqrt(q-1-mu)`, and conjugate sectors may cancel their traces.  Therefore a
new polarity theorem must control the designated square-root sectors, not
only repeat the determinant or total-trace identities already audited.

## Consequence for the cycle/ODC lane

Burgess, Cavenagh and Pike, *Mutually orthogonal cycle systems*, Ars
Mathematica Contemporanea **23** (2023), article P2.05,
DOI `10.26493/1855-3974.2692.86d`, construct families of mutually orthogonal
cyclic cycle systems of size growing with the order.  Together with general
asymptotic existence results for fixed numbers of orthogonal graph
decompositions, this confirms that three cyclic restricted owner factors and
ordinary shared-edge orthogonality are not plausible terminals by
themselves.

The missing datum is the self-indexed polarity: component vertices are also
the labels of their own restricted factors, and the cross blocks satisfy
transpose reciprocity inside the complete intermediate-factor tiling.  Any
useful cycle-system theorem must consume that datum explicitly.

## Bounded support-rank negative

There is also no immediate characteristic-two rescue from cyclicity.  If
`X` is a connected two-regular bipartite block on two shores of size
`n=2q`, then after independent row and column permutations its binary matrix
is `I+P_n`, where `P_n` is one cycle.  Over `F_2`,

```text
ker X = <1>,                 im X = 1^perp.
```

Hence a product of two cyclic blocks induces an isomorphism on
`1^perp/<1>`.  Reducing

```text
sum_e X_ce X_ed = J
```

to that quotient merely says that the `q/2` induced isomorphisms sum to
zero.  For binary `q>=8`, `q/2` is even (indeed divisible by four), so this
does not isolate or contradict a triple of cyclic colors.  A ternary
exclusion principle still needs the global self-indexed placement described
above.
