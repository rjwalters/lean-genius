The pair-capacity lemma is valid, including M=Y. A configuration consists of a middle vertex together with an unordered pair of its distinct Y-neighbors. A group element fixing this configuration fixes its middle, so regularity of the middle orbit makes the configuration action free. If two configurations have the same endpoint pair, their distinct middles and endpoints are four distinct vertices: simplicity rules out either middle being either of its own neighbors. They form a C4. Hence endpoint projection is an equivariant injection, even for middle vertices within Y. An equivariant injection preserves stabilizers, so every image orbit is free and has group order many pairs. The configuration count gives precisely binomial(b,2) free pair orbits. Images from disjoint middle orbits cannot overlap by the same C4 argument.

For regular Y, a nonidentity element stabilizing an unordered pair must swap its endpoints. After translation to{1,d}, that element is d and the swap condition is d squared equal to identity. Thus pairs whose difference is an involution have stabilizer two, and all other differences give free pairs. Translating an origin pair to another origin pair either fixes the identity endpoint or exchanges the endpoints, identifying exactly d and d inverse. This proves the claimed count of free pair orbits.
