# Forced residual incidences after the free3 S4 partial model

Assume the necessary geometry accepted2400 for the free-order3 S4 action. Write S_1,S_2,S_3 for the three A-orbits of size6 with cyclic C4 stabilizers, U for the regular24-orbit, and initially R for the three transposition-stabilizer12-orbits. The sets S_i are distinct from the three fixed sets F_t: each S_i meets each F_t in two vertices. We give a complete necessary model omitting all R-to-R edges.

Every U vertex has at mostone neighbor in a given S_i. If its common orbit-constant number were k>=2, the24 U vertices would contribute24*choose(k,2)>choose(6,2)=15 common-neighbor pairs in S_i. Its total S-degree is3 by2400, so it has exactlyone neighbor in each S_i. Consequently each S_i vertex has four U neighbors.

For r in an A-orbit of size12, its stabilizer is generated by a transposition h. It has two S-neighbors. The transposition has no fixed S vertex and therefore exchanges them. Since h preserves each A-orbit S_i, both neighbors lie in the same S_i. Equivariance makes that choice of i constant on the entire12-orbit. Such an orbit contributes four neighbors to each vertex of its chosen S_i. The matching G[S] and four U neighbors leave exactlyfour R neighbors per S_i vertex. Thus the three12-orbits attach bijectively to the three S_i. Relabel them R_i.

The map from R_i to unordered pairs of S_i, sending a vertex to its two S neighbors, is injective by C4-freeness and equivariant. In the C4-coset six-action, unordered pairs have exactly two A-orbits, of sizes3 and12. The size3 orbit consists of the canonical opposite pairs: these are the two vertices fixed by the same cyclic C4 subgroup. To see the other orbit is transitive, a generating four-cycle of the stabilizer fixes the basepoint and its opposite, and cycles the other four points (its square has the same two fixed points). Thus its stabilizer is transitive on the four non-opposite choices, giving a single unordered-pair orbit of size6*4/2=12. It follows that R_i is equivariantly and bijectively the full set of12 non-opposite pairs in S_i. Its action and S incidences are forced, with no independent subgroup or origin parameter.

## The seven possible matchings on S

Choose independent equivariant identifications of the three cyclic-C4 six-actions with a common coset action A/H. Every A-invariant matching within a copy is its unique canonical opposite matching. An equivariant bijection between two copies is determined by an element of N_A(H)/H, of size2, giving the identity identification or its opposite composition. Since every S vertex has exactlyone S neighbor, a matching between two A-orbits uses both orbits completely. Hence all possibilities are: internal opposite matching in all three copies, or an internal opposite matching in one copy and either equivariant matching between the other two. There are1+3*2=7 possibilities. They automatically stay within the double-transposition fixed sets, since equivariant maps preserve the stabilizing double transposition.

Choose the three S neighbors of the U origin, one in each S_i and one in each F_t. There are6*2^3=48 such triples under the identifications. All other incidences are their A translates. The cubic graph on U is given by any inverse-closed three-element Cayley connection set avoiding identity. A necessary42-vertex graph consists of S, U, these matching/incidence edges and the Cayley edges. Reject any C4. This paper does not run that enumeration.

## At most one residual extension of each42-vertex partial graph

By2400 each R_i vertex has two U neighbors. Edge balance with U regular shows every U vertex has exactlyone R_i neighbor for each i.

In a C4-free42-vertex graph of the preceding form, the U origin has exactlyfour distinct two-step endpoints in each S_i: one through the S matching and three through its U neighbors. The matching permutes the three S_i copies and there is one chosen S neighbor in each, so the first contribution is indeed one per S_i. Let P_i be the remaining pair of vertices in S_i. In any full graph the two-step endpoints in S are all18 vertices exactly once by2400. Its single R_i neighbor must therefore have precisely P_i as its S-neighbor pair.

If P_i is an opposite pair it is not a vertex of R_i, so the42-vertex partial graph is impossible. Otherwise the R_i neighbor is uniquely forced. Do this for each i, then translate all three choices by A. This covers every equivariant U-to-R incidence, since U is regular; no independent choices remain. Attach the forced R_i vertices to their defining pairs and perform a direct C4 check of the resulting78-vertex partial graph.

The S and U vertices then have final degree9 and the36 R vertices have degree4 (two S and two U neighbors). The five remaining neighbors per R vertex must lie in R. None of those edges is selected by this model. Negative complete enumeration would exclude the action, but positive partial graphs would still need an R completion. No enumeration outcome, full S4 exclusion, capped-domain replay or Lean formalization is asserted here.
