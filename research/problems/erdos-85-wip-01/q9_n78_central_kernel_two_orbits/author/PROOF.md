# The order-sixteen subcase with three two-orbits and kernel two

Let G be simple, C4-free and nine-regular on78 vertices, with full automorphism group A of order16. Use accepted2419: an order8 point stabilizer H has nonfree locus S of size6 inducing3K2; H acts freely outside S. The invariant sets W,R have sizes48,24, and the quotient is [[1,8,0],[1,5,3],[0,6,3]].

Consider the subcase where the A-orbits in S have sizes2+2+2 and the kernel K of A acting on S has order2. The faithful quotient A/K is then the full product C2^3 of swaps on these three orbits: it embeds into that product and has order8. In particular the three coordinate characters are distinct and independent. The matching on S pairs each two-orbit internally. Indeed an invariant matching between two different two-orbits would give an equivariant bijection between them and make their characters equal, impossible.

Write H_i for the point stabilizer of any vertex in the ith two-orbit. These are normal subgroups of index2. We first show each H_i acts freely outside S. Apply the proof of2419 using H_i in place of its initially chosen H: it only requires that H_i be an order8 stabilizer of a two-orbit. It produces a nonfree locus S_i of size6. For every s in S, its A-stabilizer has order8, and its intersection with H_i has order at least4, by the product bound in A. Hence s is nonfree for H_i, so S is contained in S_i. Their equal size gives S_i=S. This verifies the common nonfree locus, rather than assuming it from normality.

Every outside A-orbit has size8 or16. If an outside vertex has stabilizer of order2 generated by t, then t lies outside every H_i by the preceding paragraph. Therefore its image in A/K swaps each of the three S two-orbits: it is the element(1,1,1). All possible such t lie in the same coset of K, which has two elements.

There must be an outside orbit of size8, because the outside set has72 vertices and cannot be partitioned solely into16-orbits. Choose its stabilizer involution t. This t is not central: a central involution fixing one outside point would fix its entire A-orbit of size at least8, contrary to accepted2257's bound of six fixed vertices. Since A/K is abelian, all conjugates of t lie in tK. Noncentrality therefore implies its conjugacy class is exactly the two-element set tK. In particular both elements of tK are involutions, and all possible outside size8 stabilizers belong to this one conjugacy class. Its centralizer has order8.

On a size8 coset orbit A/<u>, for u conjugate to t, the element t fixes four points. This follows by counting g with g^-1 t g=u: there are |C_A(t)|=8 such g, with two representatives per coset. Consequently two outside8-orbits would give at least eight fixed vertices of t. There is exactly one outside8-orbit. The remaining64 outside vertices give four16-orbits.

Since W has size48 and is invariant, its number of size8 orbits must be even. There is only one available, so W consists of three regular16-orbits, and R consists of an8-orbit X and a regular16-orbit Y. The unique S-neighbor map sends every W16 equivariantly onto an S two-orbit, contributing eight neighbors at each of its two vertices. Each S vertex requires exactly eight W neighbors. Hence there is exactly one W16 attached to each S two-orbit. For each r in R the saturated quotient structure gives one neighbor in each of the six groups B_s, hence two in each W16. Balance then gives each W vertex one X-neighbor and two Y-neighbors.

Finally R is cubic. The order2 stabilizer of a vertex in X acts freely on Y, so the X-to-Y degree is zero or two and its internal degree is three or one. Accepted2425's elementary cubic-eight lemma excludes the degree-three alternative. Thus X induces a matching, X-to-Y degree is two, and Y-to-X degree is one, giving internal Y degree two.

The exponent of A divides4: for every g, the elementary abelian quotient A/K gives g^2 in K, whose elements square to the identity. The invariant graph on regular Y is a Cayley graph with an inverse-closed two-element connection set. An inverse pair of order4 elements gives forbidden4-cycles; two distinct involutions with product of order2 also give4-cycles. The remaining possibility is two involutions with product of order4, generating a dihedral group of order8 and giving two disjoint8-cycles on Y. This is the same elementary Cayley argument as2425, and uses no classification of groups of order16.

Therefore this specific nonfaithful S-action subcase has W=16+16+16 and R=X8+Y16, with X a matching and Y two8-cycles, cross degrees2/1. It is distinct from2422's faithful C2 x D8 S-action. This does not exclude either subcase, cover smaller S-action images, or settle graph existence. No finite graph search or Lean formalization is claimed.
