# Excluding two remaining single-four-orbit S4 patterns

Assume G is simple, C4-free and nine-regular on78 vertices, with full automorphism group A isomorphic to S4. We exclude both patterns

 (4,6,8,12,12,12,24),
 (4,6,6,6,8,12,12,24).

The complete accepted2376 character cover retains one multiset for each. Its full underlying subgroup lists give the following actions. In both cases there is a natural four-orbit F, an eight-orbit V with order3 stabilizer, two twelve-orbits B,C whose stabilizers are generated by transpositions, and one regular24-orbit U. The remaining18 vertices are:

- first pattern: one six-orbit X with cyclic C4 stabilizer and one twelve-orbit Y whose stabilizer is generated by a double transposition;
- second pattern: three six-orbits with cyclic C4 stabilizers.

For clarity, in the class order identity/transposition/three-cycle/double-transposition/four-cycle, these six and twelve characters are (6,0,0,2,2), (12,0,0,4,0), and (12,2,0,0,0). Every subgroup underlying the retained vectors has the stated type; no representative-only inference is used.

## The18 vertices form three disjoint fixed matchings

Let T be the normal Klein-four subgroup of double transpositions. No vertex stabilizer in the displayed actions contains T, so Fix(T) is empty. Every nonidentity t in T fixes six vertices, all among the18 vertices just listed. Fixed sets F_t for different t are disjoint, because two nonidentity elements generate T. Their union S is A-invariant (T is normal) and equals those18 vertices.

There are no G edges between F_t and F_s for t!=s. If x in F_t and y in F_s were adjacent, the commuting t,s would generate the four edges between {x,sx} and {y,ty}. Both pairs consist of distinct vertices since Fix(T) is empty, and the sets are disjoint. This is a C4.

Accepted2257 says G[F_t] is a matching3K2 or a triangle with three pendant leaves. Fix a four-cycle g with g^2=t.

In the second pattern g fixes all six vertices of F_t, since each cyclic-C4 six-orbit contributes two g-fixed vertices. Every g orbit outside F_t has length4: its square t fixes none of those vertices. At a fixed vertex f,9-d_{F_t}(f) is therefore divisible by4, ruling out fixed degree3 and forcing degree1. Thus G[F_t] is a matching.

In the first pattern g fixes exactly the two vertices of X lying in F_t; it fixes none in Y. Its action on F_t has order at most2. If G[F_t] were a triangle with three pendant leaves, the involution induced by g on its three cubic vertices would fix an odd number of them. But its only fixed vertices are the two X vertices. They have the same degree in G[S], since X is an A orbit and S is A-invariant. There are no inter-F_t edges, so they also have the same degree in G[F_t]. Thus either both are cubic or neither is, giving an even number of fixed cubic vertices, a contradiction. G[F_t] is a matching here as well.

Consequently G[S] is a matching on18 vertices in both cases and has exactly18*8=144 edges to its complement.

Every vertex outside S has at mostone neighbor in each F_t. Otherwise that vertex and its distinct image under t would share two F_t neighbors and form a C4. Hence every outside vertex has S-degree at most3.

## The natural four-orbit cannot supply enough capacity

Let H be a stabilizer of a vertex of F, a natural S3 subgroup. It intersects every conjugate cyclic C4 or double-transposition C2 trivially, since none of those nonidentity elements fixes a natural point. Thus all H suborbits in each orbit making up S have size6. If a six-orbit in S attached to F, an F vertex would have at least6 neighbors in it, and each six-orbit vertex at least4 F neighbors. This violates6*choose(4,2)>choose(4,2). For the twelve-orbit Y, an attachment likewise gives at least2 F neighbors per Y vertex, violating12*choose(2,2)>6. Therefore F has no S neighbors.

F is independent because its only nonempty invariant graph is K4. Edge balance and pair capacity bound its degree into V by0 or2, into B or C by0 or3, and into U by0 or6. Since its total degree is9, reduction modulo3 forces its V degree to be zero. The two twelve-orbits alone could contribute at most6, so F must attach to U and exactly one of B,C. Rename the attached twelve-orbit B. Each F vertex has six U neighbors and three B neighbors.

Let u,b,c,v denote the S-degrees of vertices in U,B,C,V, respectively. Each is constant on its A orbit and lies between0 and3. For f in F, the number of two-step paths ending in S is6u+3b. All endpoints are distinct by C4-freeness, so

 6u+3b<=18, or 2u+b<=6.

Counting the144 edges out of S, using that F has none, gives

 144=24u+12b+12c+8v
    <=12*6+12*3+8*3
    =132.

This contradiction excludes both displayed patterns.

The proof uses accepted2376's complete action cover and accepted2257's six-fixed graph classification. It performs no new graph enumeration and does not reinterpret any capped result. Other S4 actions and global N78 existence are not excluded here; no Lean formalization is claimed.
