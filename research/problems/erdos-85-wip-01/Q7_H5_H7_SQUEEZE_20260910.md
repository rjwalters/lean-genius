## Exact block constraints

All ones vectors and matrices below have the dimensions specified by context:

    BB^T=7I+J,  B1=8·1,  BC=J,
    C1=7·1-t,  Ct=h·1,  0<=t_v<=3.
    D=J+6I-B^TB-C²,
    D1=6·1-t,  DB^T=J-B^T,  Dt=h·1-t,  CD=DC.

D is a simple graph's adjacency matrix: its edges are low pairs with no
common neighbor in the full graph. Set

    a=(7+sqrt(49-4h))/2,  b=7-a,  r=a-1,  z=a·1-t.

Then z>0, Cz=az, Dz=rz, rho(D)=r<6. On
K=ker B intersect 1^perp, dimension48-2h, C²=6I-D.
Consequently no eigenvalue of C|K is zero. With psi=charpoly(C|K),

    charpoly(A)=(x²-7)^(h-1)(x³-7x²-7x+49-h)psi(x),
    charpoly(C)=x^(h-1)(x²-7x+h)psi(x).

The monic quotient psi lies in Z[x]. Its first two power sums are
-7 and294-13h. Thus its leading terms are x^38+7x^37-90x^36 for H5,
and x^34+7x^33-77x^32 for H7. A is nonsingular, and C has nullity exactly
h-1. The residual roots satisfy 6-r<=theta²<=6+r.

The rational Gram determinant square class of K is13 for H5 and35 for H7:
the complementary Gram block has determinant7^(h-1)(343-22h-h²).
This is not an assertion about an integral orthonormal basis or the exact
determinant of the integral kernel lattice. Representability of this block
alone cannot exclude a support profile: the displayed incidence masks
already realize it over Z. Joint C/D constraints are additional information.

## Defect component bookkeeping

Let S be a connected component of D; let m=|S|, T=sum_S t_v. Restrict z to S
and extend it by zero elsewhere, obtaining z_S. Since Dz_S=r z_S and
BD=J-B, we have a Bz_S=(am-T)1. Put u=B1_S and v=B(t restricted to S).
Expanding a(au-v) and using a²=7a-h gives

    a(7u-v-m·1)=h u-T·1.

As a is irrational and all remaining coefficients are integers, u=s·1
for an integer s=T/h, and v=(7s-m)·1. The value s is positive: s=0 would
force t=0 throughout S, contradicting Dt=h·1-t there.

The matrix P_S=B_S B_S^T-sI is the adjacency matrix of a simple graph on
the h high vertices. Every pair of high vertices has exactly one common
low neighbor, so its off-diagonal entries are0 or1. Its row sum is

    k=6s-m,  0<=k<=h-1.

Since h is odd, regular-graph handshake forces k even. Handshake for D[S]
gives 6m-hs even, so s is even. Across all components,

    sum s=8,  sum k=h-1,  m=6s-k.

If S contains no triple support, each high vertex has s-k singleton
supports in S, so s>=k. Equality would make every t_v in S even, whereas
(Dt)_v=h-t_v is odd. Therefore a triple-free component has s>=k+2.

## The number of defect components is odd

Work over F=Q(sqrt(49-4h)). If D has c connected components, its r-eigenspace
E has dimension c over F: z is positive on each component, so Perron's
theorem makes that eigenvalue simple on each component. The space E is
C-invariant because CD=DC. The vector z is a C-eigenvector of eigenvalue a.

For y in E, pairing D1=6·1-t with y gives <t,y>=b<1,y>. Therefore
<z,y>=(a-b)<1,y>. Thus E intersect z^perp lies in1^perp and t^perp.
Pairing DB^T=J-B^T with y also gives a By=<1,y>1, so this orthogonal
complement lies in K. It has dimension c-1 and is C-invariant, and C²=bI
on it. Now Norm_(F/Q)(b)=h is not a rational square for h=5,7. Hence b
is not a square in F and X²-b is irreducible over F. Any F-vector space
with an endomorphism satisfying C²=bI has even dimension, by viewing it
as a vector space over F[X]/(X²-b). Consequently c-1 is even.

The preceding component bookkeeping also gives 2c<=sum s=8. Thus c=1 or3.

## Consequences for the fixed support profiles

For H7/T0 every component is triple-free, so
8=sum s>=sum k+2c=6+2c. Hence c=1: D is connected.

For H5/T0, the same inequality gives c<=2. Oddness therefore forces c=1.
For either H5 profile with triples, suppose c=3. The positive even s values
must be2,2,4. The even k values sum4, giving multisets(0,0,4) or(0,2,2).
A2-regular simple graph on five vertices is C5 and contains no triangle.
Thus a component with k=2 contains no triple support, and has s>=4. The
case(0,2,2) would require sum s>=2+4+4=10 and is impossible.

In the remaining case k=4 cannot have s=2: at each high vertex,
2s-k equals twice its singleton count plus its pair-support count. Equality
to zero would force all supports in that component to be triples, with
total incidence hs=10, impossible because a triple contributes three.
Thus the remaining case has (s,k)=(2,0),(2,0),(4,4), and component orders12,12,20.
Each order12 component contains two empty masks and two copies of every
singleton. All triples lie in the order20 component. If the total number
of triples is tau, that component's support counts are

    (n0,n1,n2,n3)=(10-tau,3tau,10-3tau,tau).

For tau=0 the even-support contradiction above applies again. Therefore
H5/T1 or T2 has connected D or exactly the described12+12+20 decomposition.
These component restrictions are not nonexistence results for H5/T1 or T2.

## H7/T0 defect graph is non-bipartite

Suppose D were bipartite and let sigma take the values+1,-1 on its parts.
Then sigma*z is a -r eigenvector, orthogonal to z. Write Delta m, Delta T,
Delta U for the differences between the parts of sums of1,t,t².
The identity sum sigma_v z_v²=0 and the irrationality of a give

    7 Delta m-2 Delta T=0,  Delta U-h Delta m=0.

Equal edge counts from the two parts give 6 Delta m-Delta T=0. Hence all
three differences vanish. H7/T0 totals(m,T,U)=(42,56,98); each part would
have(T,U)=(28,49). But t²-t is even for every integer t, contradicting
U-T=21 on one part. Thus D is non-bipartite. As it is also connected, its
other eigenvalues have absolute value strictly below r. On K the r
eigenvector z is absent. The residual spectral bounds therefore sharpen to

    (7-sqrt21)/2 < theta² < (17+sqrt21)/2.

This does not exclude H7/T0. In H5 the analogous halves have(T,U)=(20,30)
and the parity argument gives no contradiction.

checked by sol1 and sol2 is (CD)_vv=7-2t_v-2tau_v. It is odd, positive, and

## H5 disconnected-pattern Gram conic: exact non-obstruction

This addendum applies sol1's [component Perron Gram construction](Q7_H3_COMPONENT_3ADIC_OBSTRUCTION_20260910.md)
to the preliminary H5 partition12+12+20. It is a separate algebraic check,
passed independent review1586; it does not produce a graph or a full spectrum.
With a²-7a+5=0, the three component Perron-vector squared norms are

    g=12a²-20a+10=64a-50  (twice),
    j=20a²-40a+40=100a-60,
    G=2g+j=228a-160.

The orthogonal complement to their sum has, up to a nonzero scalar, Gram
matrix diag(1,delta), where delta=jG=129920a-104400. As in the cited
construction, a selfadjoint operator squaring to b=7-a on this two-space
requires u²+delta v²=b over Q(a). Here that equation has the exact solution

    u=(2a-9)/4,   v=(32a-199)/2320.

Substitution and reduction by a²-7a+5 gives zero remainder. Thus this conic
has a solution over the number field and every completion, so no local
obstruction to this particular conic can eliminate12+12+20. The operator
[[u,delta*v],[v,-u]] realizes only this two-dimensional selfadjoint square
condition. It does not realize C, D, their integer lattices, or graph entries.
The setup verifier includes the exact identity and Gram-basis calculation.
The stronger integral quotient argument linked above subsequently excludes
the graph partition. This exact conic solution remains valid: it identifies
the limit of the scalar quadratic-form test, not a surviving graph case.
