# The two remaining dihedral-group actions are impossible

Assume the kernel-two frontier2477. Consider its two law100 actions: A is dihedral of order16, W consists of three regular16-orbits, and R consists of three8-orbits R0,R1,R2. We exclude both characters simultaneously. All matching-normal-form and zero-codegree premises are those of2419. Write A=<r,s | r^8=s^2=1,srs=r^-1>.

Every R_i stabilizer has order two and cannot be central by the six-fixed bound2257. It is therefore generated by a reflection. Any coset action A/<r^k s> identifies its eight points with a cyclic coordinate j modulo8: r acts by j->j+1 and the stabilizing reflection acts by j->-j. Thus every unordered pair of distinct points has a nonidentity setwise stabilizer: on coordinates a,b, the reflection j->a+b-j exchanges them. This holds for both reflection classes; it does not require the three R_i to be equivalent as A-sets.

By the free-middle lemma2438, a pair of neighbors of a vertex in a regular W orbit must have trivial setwise stabilizer. Consequently no W vertex can have two neighbors in a single R_i. Every W vertex has residual degree three by2419, so it has exactly one neighbor in each R_i. This establishes the precise hypothesis needed for the residual part of2447's argument, independently of its original S-orbit assumption. We give that argument below to make the transport explicit.

The residual quotient D=(d_ij) is symmetric, nonnegative integral and has row sums three. Each column must contain an entry at least two: otherwise no residual vertex has two neighbors in that R_i, neither does any W vertex by the preceding paragraph, and S has no residual neighbors. Its eight vertices would then be pairwise zero-codegree, giving seven zero-codegree partners per vertex rather than five. A diagonal entry three is impossible by the cubic-eight obstruction2425. An off-diagonal three would exhaust both its rows and strand the third diagonal at three, also impossible. Hence each row contains a two and a one in different positions. Symmetry makes their positions two disjoint symmetric permutation matrices on three indices; both must be distinct transpositions. Relabel to obtain

    D = [[0,2,1],[2,1,0],[1,0,2]].

The R0--R2 edges form an equivariant perfect matching, and R2 induces a transitive C4-free degree-two graph on eight vertices, hence one8-cycle. The A-action on that cycle is faithful: a nontrivial kernel would be an order-two subgroup in a point stabilizer and would fix all eight vertices, violating2257. Thus A acts as the full dihedral automorphism group of the cycle. Each of its four vertex-stabilizer subgroups fixes an opposite pair. Through the R0--R2 matching, it fixes two vertices in R0 as well.

There are exactly16 pairs in R0 x R2 with nontrivial stabilizer, counted as four subgroups times two fixed points in each orbit. The other48 pairs form three free A-orbits. Each regular W orbit maps injectively and equivariantly to these cross-pairs by its unique neighbors in R0 and R2. Injectivity and disjointness of the three images follow from C4-freeness. They therefore exhaust all48 free pairs.

For m in R2, let u be its matching neighbor in R0 and v either of its cycle neighbors in R2. These16 incidences form a free A-orbit: the reflection fixing m swaps its two cycle neighbors. Forgetting m is injective by C4-freeness and produces another16 free cross-pairs, disjoint from all the W images because the middles lie in R2. This exceeds the capacity48, a contradiction.

Thus both law100 actions are excluded. In the kernel-two cover2477 only law111 with characters(0,1) and(1,0) remains; those are the semidihedral group with noncentral involutions projecting to edge-axis reflections on S4. The kernel-four cases remain separate, and neither their exclusion nor a full order16/global graph conclusion is claimed. No finite enumeration, capped retry or Lean formalization is used. Independent review is requested.
