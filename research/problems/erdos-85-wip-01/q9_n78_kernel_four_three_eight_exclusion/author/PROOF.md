# Exclusion of kernel-four residual8+8+8

Assume the full order16 matching normal form2419, S-orbits2+4, and S-action kernel of order four. Consider the residual profile R=R0 union R1 union R2 with each orbit of size eight. Accepted2451 gives three regular W16 orbits whose R-degree rows, after labeling, are W0:(1,1,1), W1:(2,1,0), W2:(0,1,2). Accepted2438 supplies a central involution z fixing exactly S. Every residual point stabilizer has order two, generated by a noncentral involution t, and z is independent of t. Thus its conjugacy class has size two or four: its centralizer contains <z,t> of order four, while a central stabilizer would fix an entire eight-orbit, violating2257.

## No residual internal degree two

The W1 neighborhood in R0 has size two. The free-middle lemma2438 injects its sixteen middle/pair incidences into a free orbit of unordered R0 pairs. Similarly R2 has a free unordered pair orbit from W2.

The group A cannot be dihedral of order16. Indeed in any transitive eight-point action of that group the point stabilizer cannot be the central involution, and hence is generated by a reflection. Each of the two reflection-coset actions is equivalent to the natural eight-cycle action after choosing generators: identify r^i<t> with i modulo eight, where r acts by i->i+1 and the stabilizing reflection acts by i->-i. Every unordered pair {i,j} is interchanged by the reflection x->i+j-x. Thus no unordered pair is free, contradicting the preceding R0 pair orbit.

An invariant internal degree-two graph on any Ri is a union of equal-length cycles, by transitivity. Cycle lengths divide eight and are at least three; C4-freeness leaves only one8-cycle. The action of A on Ri is faithful: its kernel is a normal subgroup contained in the order-two point stabilizer, and a nontrivial such kernel would make that involution central. Hence A embeds into Aut(C8), of order16, and must equal that dihedral group. This contradiction excludes internal degree two. Internal degree three is excluded by the accepted cubic-eight obstruction2425. Therefore every internal degree d_i is zero or one.

## All three stabilizer classes coincide and have size four

If two residual orbits have different stabilizer conjugacy classes, a point stabilizer on one acts freely on the other. Their cross-degree is therefore even. Write a_ij for cross-degrees. Equal orbit sizes give a_ij=a_ji, and each row satisfies d_i+sum_{j!=i} a_ij=3.

If all three classes differ, every d_i must be one and every cross-degree is even, summing to two in each row. Dividing the cross-degrees by two would give a degree-one simple graph on three indices, impossible.

If exactly two classes coincide, label those indices i,j and the third k. Both a_ik and a_jk are even; the k row forces d_k=1 and these two cross-degrees to be zero and two in some order. Subtracting the i and j row equations gives d_i-d_j+a_ik-a_jk=0. The first difference is between minus one and one, while the second is plus or minus two, impossible.

Thus all three use one conjugacy class. It cannot have size two: each involution of such a class fixes four vertices in each orbit, and in Ri x Rj its fixed pairs give sixteen nonfree pairs. The two distinct involutions have disjoint fixed sets, because every point stabilizer has order two. Consequently there are32 nonfree and only32 free cross pairs. But between R0 and R1, W0 supplies16 free middle/pair incidences and W1 supplies32, all distinct by C4-freeness. This needs48 free pairs. Therefore the common class has size four.

Each involution of this class fixes exactly two points in each Ri (centralizer order four divided by stabilizer order two). Between any two residual orbits there are consequently16 nonfree and48 free pairs. The W0/W1 incidences exhaust all48 free pairs between R0,R1; W0/W2 likewise exhaust all48 free pairs between R1,R2.

## Saturated cross pairs force six-vertex cubic pieces

Suppose a residual vertex x has neighbors y in R0 and y' in R1, or neighbors in R1 and R2. Their pair already has a W middle if free, which would contradict C4-freeness. It must be nonfree. A nonidentity element fixing both endpoints fixes their unique common neighbor x. Since all three point stabilizers have order two, their stabilizers coincide.

In particular, if x has a neighbor in R1 and any neighbor in R0 or R2, all neighbors of x have the same stabilizer as x: pair each neighbor outside R1 with one in R1, and pair each neighbor in R1 with one outside it.

The row equations with all d_i in {0,1} leave exactly the following degree patterns. The sum d_0+d_1+d_2 is odd, so either all are one, giving every a_ij=1, or exactly one d_k is one. In the latter case the two cross-degrees incident to k are one and the cross-degree between the other two indices is two. This follows directly by adding and subtracting the three row equations.

If all d_i are one, every vertex has one neighbor in each orbit and satisfies the preceding stabilizer-propagation condition. If only d_1 is one, every vertex again has a neighbor in R1 and outside R1. If only d_0 is one, vertices in R0 and R2 satisfy the condition; every edge incident to R1 has its other endpoint in R0 or R2, and so it too joins equal stabilizers. The case only d_2=1 is symmetric. Hence in every case all residual edges join vertices with identical point stabilizers.

For each of the four stabilizer involutions there are exactly two corresponding vertices in each Ri, giving a six-vertex fiber. Every residual edge remains inside a fiber, and R is cubic. Each fiber would therefore be a simple cubic C4-free graph on six vertices. Its six vertices supply6*binomial(3,2)=18 unordered neighbor pairs with distinguished middles, but only binomial(6,2)=15 endpoint pairs exist. Two middles for one endpoint pair give a4-cycle. Contradiction.

The entire kernel-four residual8+8+8 profile is excluded. This proof is independent of the separate residual8+16 exclusion2479. Combining them requires independent acceptance of both. No finite search or Lean formalization is used, and this packet alone makes no kernel-two, smaller-automorphism-order, or global graph-existence claim.
