# Excluding S4 orbit sizes (4,6,6,6,8,24,24)

Let G be simple, C4-free and nine-regular on78 vertices, A=Aut(G) isomorphic to S4, with the displayed orbit sizes. We use accepted2257's bound of six fixed vertices for any involution and elementary S4 actions. This proof excludes the whole size pattern, not just one fixed-count tuple.

The four-orbit F carries the natural action: a point stabilizer has order6, normalizes its unique Sylow3 subgroup, and equals that three-cycle subgroup's natural point stabilizer. F is independent since its only nonempty invariant graph is K4.

## The six-orbit types

A size6 orbit has stabilizer of order4, either cyclic C4 or Klein four. A Klein four with no transposition is the normal subgroup T of double transpositions. A Klein four containing a transposition is generated by two disjoint transpositions, since its other elements commute with that transposition; call this type B. The B action is the natural S4 action on the six unordered pairs of four letters. The C4 action is the action on six directed cyclic orderings of those letters, considered up to rotation.

For a double transposition d, the centralizer has order8. A cyclic C4 or a type B subgroup contains one double transposition, whereas T contains three. The fixed-coset formula gives respectively2,2,6 fixed vertices in these six-orbits. Thus the three six-orbits already contribute at least6, and the bound2257 excludes any T-type orbit.

For a transposition t, the centralizer has order4. A B stabilizer contains two transpositions and contributes two fixed vertices; a cyclic C4 contains none and contributes zero. The natural four-orbit contributes two fixed vertices; stabilizers of orders3 and1 in the8/24 orbits cannot contain t. If m of the three six-orbits have type B, then t fixes2+2m vertices. Hence m<=2.

At f in F, the natural S3 stabilizer has trivial intersection with every conjugate C4 (neither a four-cycle nor a double transposition fixes a natural point). Thus its suborbits in C4-type six-orbits have size6. Its suborbits in the8-orbit have size2 or6, and in regular24-orbits size6. These are all even. Since f has odd degree and F is independent, f must have a neighbor in some B-type orbit B.

By edge balance and pair capacity this B has exactlytwo F neighbors per vertex: writing q for F-to-B degree and r for B-to-F degree,4q=6r, so r is even;6*choose(r,2)<=6 gives r<=2, and the attachment is nonempty. Fix this orbit B. We bound all its other neighbor counts.

## Neighbors of the selected B orbit

Within B, the invariant relations between distinct two-element subsets are disjointness (degree1) and intersection in one letter (degree4). The degree4 graph or its degree5 union violates the necessary C4 pair bound6*choose(d,2)<=15. Thus the internal B degree is at mostone.

Between B and another B-type six-orbit, after an equivariant identification the relations have degrees1,1,4: equality, complementation, and intersection in one letter. Pair capacity bounds the bipartite degree by2, eliminating the degree4 relation. The union of the two degree1 relations has degree2 but gives a K2,2 on any complementary pair and its two copies. Hence the cross degree is at mostone.

There are no edges from B to a C4-type six-orbit. In the directed cyclic ordering model, the stabilizer of a two-element subset e has two orbits on the six directed cycles: the two cycles in which e is a diagonal, and the four cycles in which e is an edge. One can see this by fixing e={1,2}: swapping its letters and swapping the other two letters act transitively on each of those two sets. Pair capacity again limits a cross degree to2, leaving only the diagonal relation. But e and its complementary pair are diagonals of exactly the same two directed cycles, so that relation gives a K2,2 and is forbidden.

There are no edges from B to the8-orbit. The order4 stabilizer of a B vertex acts freely there, since point stabilizers in that orbit have order3. A nonzero neighbor count would therefore be at least4 and a multiple of4. By balance the8-orbit vertices would have at least3 B neighbors. This violates pair capacity:8*choose(3,2)=24>choose(6,2)=15.

Finally, the neighbor count in either regular24-orbit is divisible by4, since the order4 B stabilizer acts freely.

## Degree contradiction

A vertex of B has two F neighbors, at mostone internal B neighbor, at mostone neighbor in each of the other m-1 B-type orbits, none in the C4-type or8-orbits, and a multiple of4 neighbors in the regular24-orbits. Thus its total degree is

 2+epsilon+4k, with 0<=epsilon<=m<=2.

The possible residues modulo4 are2,3,0, never1. This contradicts degree9.

The argument uses no finite character enumeration or capped graph search; its action models and pair bounds are explicit. It excludes only this S4 orbit-size pattern. Other S4 actions and global N78 existence remain unresolved, and no Lean formalization is claimed.
