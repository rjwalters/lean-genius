# Further restriction on the kernel-four residual8+16 profile

Assume the N78 simple C4-free9-regular graph has full automorphism group A of order16, S-orbits2+4, S-action kernel of order four, and residual orbits X8 plus regularY16. Accepted2451 gives three regular W16 orbits with Y-degrees2,1,3 respectively, internalY degree two, and X-to-Y degree two. This packet is conditional on that explicit profile and does not address R8+8+8.

Apply the accepted2438 free-middle pair lemma to the three W orbits and to Y itself. Their images on unordered Y pairs are disjoint, and require binomial(2,2)+binomial(1,2)+binomial(3,2)+binomial(2,2)=5 free A-orbits. For a regular A action on Y, these are indexed by inverse pairs of elements whose order is greater than two. Consequently A has at least ten such elements and at most five involutions among its fifteen nonidentity elements.

The invariant degree-two graph on Y consists of cycles of equal length, because A is transitive on Y. A simple cycle has length at least three, and equal component sizes divide sixteen. The possibilities are lengths four, eight or sixteen; C4-freeness excludes four. Hence Y induces either two8-cycles or one16-cycle.

Suppose it is a16-cycle. The regular action of A on Y is faithful, so it embeds as an order16 subgroup of Aut(C16), a dihedral group of order32. Write its rotations as r, of order16, and a reflection as s. If A lies entirely in the rotation subgroup, equality of orders gives A=C16. This is impossible: the point stabilizer of X has a nonidentity involution, which would be central and fix all eight X vertices, contradicting the six-fixed bound2257.

Otherwise A has an element outside the rotations. Its intersection with the rotations has index two in A and order eight, hence is the subgroup generated by r squared. The remaining coset has representative r^k s. Thus A is generated by r squared and r^k s, and is a dihedral group of order16. (Transitivity on the full16-cycle additionally forces k odd, though this is not needed for the count.) Its eight reflections are involutions; among its eight rotations, only the identity and the half-turn have order at most two. It therefore has exactly six elements of order greater than two, contradicting the necessary lower bound ten.

The16-cycle alternative is impossible. Thus the kernel-four8+16 profile requires Y to induce exactly two8-cycles, and A to have at most five involutions. These conditions do not provide a graph realization or a full subcase exclusion. No finite search, general classification of groups of order16 or Lean formalization is used. Independent review is requested.
