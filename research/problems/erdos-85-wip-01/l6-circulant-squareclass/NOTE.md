# A combined determinant and modulo-two restriction for cyclic defects

Let q=2^k with k≥2 and n=q². Suppose a hypothetical q-regular C4-free
square-order graph has a **connected circulant** defect D, whose connection
set is

    {n/2} union ±R,   R subset {1,...,n/2-1},   |R|=(q-2)/2.

Let r be the number of odd elements of R. Then necessarily

    r=4s² for some positive integer s.                    (1)

No circulant or equivariance assumption is made on the hypothetical
adjacency square root A. This is a restriction on a particular defect
class, not a proof that arbitrary defects belong to that class.

## Determinant factorization over the integers

Put M=(q-1)I+J-D and let S be cyclic shift. Reflection i↦-i splits Q^n
into V+ and V-, of dimensions n/2+1 and n/2-1. All the matrices in question
commute with reflection or have the stated intertwinement below.

The map T=S-S^-1 sends V+ to V- and commutes with M. Its kernel on V+
consists precisely of the two-periodic vectors: the constant vector1 and
the alternating vector ε_i=(-1)^i. Its rank is therefore n/2-1, making it
surjective onto V-. The two kernel eigenvalues of M are

    M1=n1,       Mε=4r ε.

For the second equation, the antipodal step is even, and the paired steps
give Dε=(q-1-4r)ε. The all-ones matrix kills ε.

The induced action of M on V+/ker T is conjugate to its action on V-.
Let P=det(M|V-). The determinant on V+ is n·4r·P, and hence

    det M = 4q² r P².                                    (2)

Crucially, P is an integer. The lattice generated by
 e_i-e_(n-i), 1≤i<n/2, is invariant under the integral matrix M, and these
vectors form a basis of V-. Thus its restriction has an integer matrix.

Connectedness makes M=L_D+J positive definite, so P≠0. Also r>0: if all
representatives were even, every step including n/2 would be even and the
circulant would be disconnected. Since M=A², det M is an integer square.
Equation (2) then forces the integer r to be a square, by prime valuations.

## Combining with the earlier obstruction

The proposition in NONBIP_CONNECTED_2ADIC_TERMINAL_AUDIT.md, section
“A modulo-two square-root obstruction for cyclic defects”, excludes odd r:
M modulo two is nilpotent with nullity2 and admits no alternating square
root. This is independent of a putative root's automorphisms.

Therefore r is both even and a positive square, proving (1). Because
r≤(q-2)/2, this excludes connected circulant defects for q=4 or8. At q=16
or32 the only surviving value is r=4. The conclusion does not address
circulant defects that are disconnected, or arbitrary noncirculant defects.

The new part of this note is the explicit reflection determinant
factorization and its combination with the existing modulo-two criterion.
No literature novelty or new Lean formalization is claimed.

## Exact q16 checks

check.py constructs the integer plus/minus restrictions, verifies the
intertwiner and both distinguished eigenvectors, and computes their exact
determinants. It checks det(M|V+)=n·4r·P and hence (2), without floating
point eigenvalues or factoring a huge integer.

| R | r | valuation of M(1+z) modulo2 | det M square? |
|---|---|---|---|
| {1,2,4,6,8,10,12} | 1 | 2 | yes |
| {1,2,3,4,5,6,7} | 4 | 8 | yes |

The first is the memo's already-excluded D16. The second passes the two
particular screening tests just discussed. This does **not** prove that the
second matrix has an alternating square root modulo two, is represented by
I over Z2, has a rational symmetric root, or comes from a graph A.
No lattice test or graph search was performed here.

## A cofinal family passing these two particular screens

For q=4^a, a≥2, take R={1,...,q/2-1}. It has r=q/4, which is an even
square, so the determinant in (2) is a square. The step1 gives connectedness.
Put m=q/2. In the Laurent polynomial ring over F2,

    sum_(j=1)^(m-1) (X^j+X^-j)
      = (X^m+1)(X^(m-1)+1)/(X^(m-1)(X+1)).

At X=1 this has valuation m, because m is a power of two and m-1 is odd.
The two other terms of the full M polynomial are 1+X^(n/2), of valuation
n/2, and J, of valuation n-1. Thus M's valuation is exactly q/2, rather
than2, and the earlier nullity-two obstruction does not apply.

This family shows that the combined screens do not exclude every cyclic
defect on a cofinal range. It supplies a clearly scoped replacement input
if the lattice method is pursued later; it does not pass all modulo-two
root conditions and is not an A-REG control.

Run `python3 check.py`. verification.json contains exact block determinants,
intertwiner ranks, and polynomial valuations for the two q16 examples.
