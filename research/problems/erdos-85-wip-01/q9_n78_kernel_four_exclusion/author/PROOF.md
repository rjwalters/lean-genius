# Exclusion of the three-S2 action with kernel four

Assume the hypotheses and necessary geometry of the separately submitted three-pair-geometry packet. In particular |A|=16, |K|=4, W is three regular16-orbits W0,W1,W2, and R is three eight-point orbits R0,R1,R2. Every W vertex has exactly one neighbor in each R_i. All three R_i have conjugate order-two stabilizers from one conjugacy class of size four. Each such involution fixes exactly two vertices in each R_i. All arguments below are conditional on independent acceptance of that geometry packet.

Fix distinct i,j. The Cartesian product R_i times R_j has64 ordered pairs, with endpoints distinguished by their different orbits. A pair has nontrivial stabilizer precisely when its endpoint stabilizers are equal: both are order-two subgroups, so their intersection is either trivial or that subgroup. Each of the eight vertices of R_i has exactly two partners of the same stabilizer in R_j. There are16 nonfree pairs and48 free pairs.

For each regular middle orbit W_k, associate to every w its unique R_i-neighbor and unique R_j-neighbor. This is an equivariant map into R_i times R_j. It is injective: two distinct W middles with the same two endpoints would form a C4. Its image therefore consists of16 free pairs, since an equivariant injection from a regular orbit has trivial image stabilizers. Images of different W_k are disjoint by the same C4 argument. The three W orbits consume all48 free pairs.

It follows that two neighbors of any residual middle r in R, lying in distinct residual orbits R_i,R_j, must have equal stabilizers. Otherwise they would form a free pair already having a W common neighbor, and that W vertex together with r would produce a C4.

Let d_ij be the number of R_j-neighbors of a vertex in R_i. This is well-defined by transitivity. All R_i have size eight, so edge balance gives d_ij=d_ji. Every row sums to three because R is cubic. No row can have just one positive entry. A diagonal entry three would make a C4-free cubic graph on eight vertices, excluded by the elementary lemma2425. An off-diagonal entry d_ij=3 also forces d_ji=3; those two rows are saturated, so the remaining orbit R_k has internal degree three, again impossible. Hence every residual vertex has neighbors in at least two residual orbits.

A cubic neighborhood meeting at least two invariant orbits has a singleton intersection with one of them. Let u be such a unique neighbor of r. Its uniqueness implies A_r fixes u, so A_r is contained in A_u; both have order two, hence equality. Every other neighbor of r in a different residual orbit has the same stabilizer as u by the preceding pair argument. If a further neighbor lies in u's orbit it would violate the singleton choice; if a different orbit supplies two neighbors, each separately forms a cross-orbit pair with u. Therefore every R-edge joins vertices with equal point stabilizers.

There are four possible stabilizer subgroups. For each, the corresponding vertex set contains exactly two points from each R_i, hence six vertices total. Every R-edge stays within one such six-point set. Since R is cubic, each set induces a simple C4-free cubic graph on six vertices. This is impossible: from any vertex there are3*2=6 nonreturning length-two walks with distinct endpoints by C4-freeness, but only five other vertices in that set.

Thus the entire three-S2/kernel-four action is impossible. The proof uses exact equivariant incidence counting, without enumerating residual edges or full graph completions. It excludes neither the kernel-eight three-S2 action nor different S-orbit patterns. It is not a Lean formalization or a global Erdős85 result. Independent review is requested, with the geometry dependency explicit.
