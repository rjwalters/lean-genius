# Excluding the C2 x C4 center-image action with kernel two

Assume the N78 simple C4-free nine-regular graph has full automorphism group A of order16 and the S2+S4 matching-center pattern of2419. Suppose its action kernel K has order2 and its image B is C2 x C4: the first factor swaps S2 and the second acts regularly on S4. This is an explicit subcase hypothesis; the proof does not depend on the pending image enumeration2450.

Write K=<z>. It is central because its unique nonidentity element is fixed by every conjugation. Choose lifts a,b of the C2 and C4 factors. Every element has a unique form a^i b^j z^e with i,e binary and 0<=j<4. Since the quotient is abelian, there are bits alpha,beta,gamma such that

    a^2=z^alpha, b^4=z^beta, a b a^-1=b z^gamma.

These relations specify the multiplication on all16 normal forms. The square of a b^j is z^(alpha+gamma*j) b^(2j). The element z acts trivially on S, a swaps S2 and fixes S4, and b fixes S2 and rotates S4 by one step.

## Only two extension cases can remain

Let H be the index-two stabilizer of an S2 point. Accepted2419 makes H free outside S, and all outside A-orbits have size8 or16. Since their total size is72, at least one has size8. Its point stabilizer is generated by an involution t outside H. If A were abelian, this involution would fix that entire eight-orbit, contradicting2257's bound of six fixed vertices. Thus gamma=1: gamma=0 would make the generators a,b,z commute.

The image of t is an involution in B swapping S2, so it is either aK or ab^2K. An involution in aK fixes all four S4 vertices. Its conjugacy class has size at most2 because B is abelian and K has size2; a central such involution cannot fix an outside orbit at all. If noncentral its centralizer has size8, so on an outside eight-orbit with its conjugate stabilizer it fixes8/2=4 vertices. Together with the four S4 points this exceeds six. Hence no outside point stabilizer can have its involution in aK.

Therefore t lies in ab^2K. Its square is z^(alpha+beta), so alpha=beta is necessary. The remaining cases are (alpha,beta)=(0,0) and(1,1), with gamma=1. In either case the two involutions in ab^2K are conjugate, since conjugating by b changes the z factor. They are the only possible outside stabilizer class. Each fixes four points per eight-orbit; two such orbits would exceed the bound six. Thus there is exactly one outside eight-orbit and four sixteen-orbits.

All W point stabilizers are trivial. A nontrivial one would be an outside involution of the allowed class ab^2K, but this class swaps S2 and acts on S4 by the nontrivial half-turn, fixing no S point. It cannot fix the unique S-neighbor of a fixed W vertex. Consequently W is three regular16-orbits W0,W1,W2 and R=X8+Y16. The unique-center map attaches one W16 to S2 and two to S4, as in2425's elementary fiber count.

R is cubic. Its X point stabilizer acts freely on regular Y, making X-to-Y degree even. The cubic-eight lemma2425 excludes internal X degree3, so X-to-Y degree is2, Y-to-X degree1, and Y internal degree2.

## The W-to-Y degree possibilities

Let c_i be W_i-to-Y degree, which equals Y-to-W_i degree by equal sizes and transitivity. The W0 orbit over S2 is the union of the two corresponding center-neighbor groups. Every residual vertex meets each such group once, so c0=2. Every Y vertex has six W-neighbors, giving c1+c2=4. Each W vertex has total R-degree3 by2419, so 0<=c_i<=3. Thus (c1,c2) is (1,3),(2,2), or(3,1).

If all three c_i equal2, every vertex outside S has exactly two Y-neighbors: this holds on X and Y by the preceding cubic structure, and on W by c_i=2. For any y in Y, its nine neighbors give at most nine other Y endpoints of a two-step walk. At least six of the other fifteen Y vertices then have zero codegree with y, contradicting the exact global zero-codegree degree5. This is the nine-endpoint argument of2441, with its hypotheses explicitly established here. Therefore one of W1,W2 must have Y-degree3.

## Case (alpha,beta)=(0,0)

Here b has order4, z is independent of b^2, and the square formula gives exactly eight noninvolutions, all of order4. Explicitly b^j z^e with j odd gives four; a b^j z^e with j odd gives four more, while all nonidentity elements with j even are involutions.

The free-middle pair lemma of accepted2438 says a regular middle orbit having c Y-neighbors needs binomial(c,2) distinct free A-orbits of unordered Y-pairs. Different middle orbits give disjoint images. The regular Y middle orbit itself needs one such pair orbit because its internal degree is2. A regular A-action with eight noninvolutions has exactly four free unordered-pair orbits, indexed by inverse pairs of noninvolutions.

But the necessary degree pattern (2,1,3), in either order for the last two, requires 1+0+3=4 pair orbits from W, plus one from Y, totaling five. This is impossible. The alternative (2,2,2) was already excluded by zero-codegree counting. Hence this extension case is excluded.

## Case (alpha,beta)=(1,1)

Here b has order8 and z=b^4. Put t=ab^2. Then t is an involution and t b t=b^5. All elements can equivalently be written b^j t^e for 0<=j<8, e binary. Their squares are b^(2j) when e=0 and b^(6j) when e=1. Thus the only involutions are b^4,t,b^4t, and these commute. The other elements have order4 or8. Every order8 element c has c^2 equal to b^2 or b^6.

Since Y is regular and internally degree2, its graph is a Cayley graph with an inverse-closed two-element connection set. Two distinct involutions here commute and yield forbidden four-cycles. An inverse pair of order4 elements also yields four-cycles. The only remaining choice is {c,c^-1} for an order8 element c, giving two eight-cycles. The unordered pairs of Y-neighbors of a Y middle then have difference c^2 or c^-2, namely b^2 or b^6. They fill the unique free unordered-pair orbit corresponding to {b^2,b^6}.

Choose the W orbit attached to S4 with Y-degree3, whose existence was established above. Identify Y with A, choose an origin w in this regular W orbit, and let D={d1,d2,d3} be its three Y-neighbors. Its S-neighbor is an S4 point s. The Y identity vertex has the three W-neighbors d1^-1 w,d2^-1 w,d3^-1 w, with S-neighbors d1^-1 s,d2^-1 s,d3^-1 s. These S-neighbors must all differ, since otherwise a Y vertex and an S center would have two common W-neighbors. Hence the C4 image coordinates of d1,d2,d3 are three distinct residues modulo4.

Any three distinct residues modulo4 contain an opposite pair. For the corresponding d_i,d_j, the group difference d_i^-1 d_j has S4 coordinate2. In the original a^i b^j z^e normal forms, these coordinate-two elements are exactly b^2,b^6,ab^2,ab^2z. The last two are involutions; the first two constitute the inverse pair {b^2,b^6}.

The involution-difference possibility is impossible: such an unordered Y-pair has a nontrivial stabilizer, whereas a pair with a common middle in a regular W orbit must have trivial stabilizer by the injective free-middle lemma. The other possibility is also impossible: that pair belongs to the free pair orbit already filled by the internal Y-middle incidences. It would have both a W common neighbor and a Y common neighbor, creating a C4. Thus no W orbit can have three Y-neighbors, contradicting the required degree pattern. This excludes the second extension case.

Both possibilities are impossible, so the entire kernel-two C2 x C4 center-image subcase is excluded.
