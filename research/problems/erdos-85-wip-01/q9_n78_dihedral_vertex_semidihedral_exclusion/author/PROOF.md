# Exclusion of the vertex-reflection semidihedral lift

Assume the full order16 matching normal form2419 and a kernel-two dihedral center image. Consider specifically the law (a,b,c)=(1,0,1) of2470. It has presentation A=<r,s | r^8=s^2=1, srs=r^3>, with z=r^4 generating the center-action kernel. On S4, r is a quarter-turn and s a vertex-axis reflection. Either nontrivial S2 character admitting an outside involution is covered. The all-regular W conclusion of separately submitted2472 is an explicit independent-review premise below.

The only involutions are r^4 and r^(2i)s for i=0,1,2,3. Indeed (r^j s)^2=r^(4j). The latter four form one conjugacy class and project to vertex-axis reflections; the central involution cannot stabilize an outside orbit. Thus every outside eight-orbit has that same class of point stabilizer. Such an involution fixes two S4 centers and exactly two points on each eight-orbit, since its centralizer has order4. Three residual eight-orbits would give at least2+3*2=8 fixed vertices, contrary to2257. Therefore R=X8 union Y16.

By2472, W consists of three regular16-orbits, one W0 attached to S2 and W1,W2 attached to S4. Cubic residual degree and the elementary cubic-eight obstruction in2425 imply that X induces a matching, each X vertex has two Y-neighbors, and Y has internal degree two: the involutory X stabilizer acts freely on Y, forcing even cross-degree, and internal X degree three is impossible.

Every S vertex has zero-codegree partners exactly the other five S vertices by2419's E[S]=K6 and global zero-codegree degree five. Thus each vertex of X shares exactly one neighbor with each S2 vertex. All their possible common neighbors lie in W0, and each W0 vertex has exactly one S-neighbor. Each X vertex consequently has two W0-neighbors. Balancing the X8/W0_16 incidence gives one X-neighbor at every W0 vertex and hence two Y-neighbors, using W-to-R degree three.

Write b_i for W_i-to-Y degree. Equal orbit sizes give the same reverse degree. We have b0=2, b1+b2=4 and 0<=b_i<=3. If b1=b2=2, every vertex outside S has two Y-neighbors. A vertex y in Y then has at most nine other Y vertices sharing a neighbor, because its nine neighbors each supply only one other Y endpoint. At least six of the other fifteen Y vertices have zero codegree with y, contradicting degree five. Hence (b1,b2) is (1,3) or (3,1). In particular a regular W orbit attached to S4 has three Y-neighbors at each vertex.

Identify regular Y with A under left translation. Its degree-two graph has an inverse-closed connection set T of size two. If T={a,a^-1}, the order of a is at least three. Every element has order1,2,4 or8; order4 gives C4 components, so a has order8. The only order8 elements are odd powers of r, since odd r^j s have order4. The unordered Y pairs sharing a middle in Y therefore form the free pair orbit with difference {r^2,r^6}.

Otherwise T consists of two distinct involutions. If one is the central r^4, they commute and give C4 components. For two noncentral involutions r^(2i)s,r^(2j)s, their product is r^(2i+6j). A product of order2 again gives C4 components. The remaining possibility is order4, namely product r^2 or r^6. This gives the same unordered-pair orbit {r^2,r^6}. Thus every allowed Y connection set uses that pair orbit for its internal length-two paths.

Now choose an origin w in the W orbit with three Y-neighbors D={d1,d2,d3}, and let its S4-neighbor be c. At the Y identity, the corresponding W-neighbors are d_i^-1 w and their S4-neighbors are d_i^-1 c. These three S4 points are distinct: a repeated one would have two common W-neighbors with the Y identity, a C4. Three distinct square vertices contain an opposite pair. Choose its index i as the origin by translating w and D by d_i^-1. The two relevant Y neighbors become1 and e=d_i^-1 d_j, and the attachment center becomes c'=d_i^-1 c. The points c' and e^-1 c' are opposite.

An element of the square dihedral group sending a vertex to its opposite is either the half-turn or the vertex-axis reflection through the other opposite pair. Therefore e lies either in {r^2,r^6}, or among the vertex-reflection lifts r^(2k)s, all involutions. In the first case the pair {1,e} already has a middle in Y by the previous paragraph and now has the distinct middle d_i^-1 w in W, a C4. In the second case {1,e} has a nontrivial stabilizer in the regular Y action, impossible for a pair with a middle in a regular W orbit by the injective equivariant free-middle lemma2438.

Both possibilities contradict C4-freeness. Hence this entire vertex-reflection law is excluded for the kernel-two dihedral image, once2472 is accepted. This does not exclude the differently labeled law (1,1,1), whose involutions project to edge-axis reflections, or the other extension laws. No finite graph search, capped retry, global graph exclusion or Lean formalization is used. Independent review is requested.
