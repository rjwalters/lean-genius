## Graph identities

Let A be a symmetric zero-diagonal 0/1 adjacency matrix of order49, with no C4 and minimum degree7. Nonbacktracking length-two walks from a vertex have distinct endpoints other than that vertex, giving sum over neighbors of (degree-1) at most48. Therefore all degrees are7 or8. Write h for the number of degree8 vertices.

A degree8 vertex has only degree7 neighbors and exactly one common neighbor with every other vertex. High vertices are independent. With high vertices first, write A=[[0,B],[B^T,C]], low order l=49-h, and t=B^T 1_h. Then

```
B 1_l = 8 1_h,   BB^T = 7I_h+J_h,   BC = J_(h,l),
C 1_l = 7 1_l-t,  Ct = h 1_l.
```

Here C is itself symmetric, zero-diagonal and 0/1. Each neighborhood induces a matching: two adjacent edges within it would make a C4. Every high neighbor of a low vertex must be matched to a different low neighbor. Consequently t_v<=3.

The missing-common-neighbor graph restricted to lows has adjacency

```
D = J_l+6I_l-B^T B-C²,
D 1_l = 6 1_l-t,   Dt = h 1_l-t,
DB^T = J_(l,h)-B^T,   CD=DC.
```

These are joint identities for the original integer matrices, not arbitrary spectral relaxations.

## Exact factors and nonsingularity

The high-difference space and its image under B^T contribute h-1 pairs of eigenvalues ±sqrt(7) to A, and h-1 zero eigenvalues to C. The span of 1_l,t has C quotient [[7,h],[-1,0]]. The remaining rational orthogonal space K=ker(B) intersect 1_l-perp has dimension48-2h and is C-invariant. Thus, with a monic integer polynomial psi_h,

```
char_C(x) = x^(h-1) (x²-7x+h) psi_h(x),
char_A(x) = (x²-7)^(h-1) (x³-7x²-7x+49-h) psi_h(x).
```

Integrality of psi follows by division of the monic integer characteristic polynomial by its monic integer forced factors. This does not assert that C|K is an integer matrix in an orthonormal basis.

Set a=(7+sqrt(49-4h))/2. For h=1,3 we have a>6 and t<=3, so w=a 1_l-t is strictly positive. Directly Dw=(a-1)w. For a nonnegative matrix with a strictly positive eigenvector, its spectral radius is a-1 (conjugate by diag(w) to obtain constant row sums). Hence rho(D)=a-1<6. On K, C²=6I-D, so every residual eigenvalue lambda satisfies

```
6-rho(D) <= lambda² <= 6+rho(D).
```

In particular psi_h(0) is nonzero. The other forced factors also have nonzero constant terms, so **A is nonsingular**. Its determinant is a nonzero multiple of48 when h=1 and2254 when h=3. This strengthens the earlier draft's divisibility-only observation.

### Joint local triangle/defect identity (review #1575 PASS)

At a low vertex v, let tau_v count all-low triangles containing v and put k_v=(CD)_(v,v). Among its7-t_v low neighbors, exactly t_v share a high common neighbor and exactly2 tau_v share a low common neighbor. C4-freeness makes these disjoint and exhaustive for edges having a common neighbor. Hence

```
k_v = 7-2t_v-2tau_v,   0<=k_v<=6-t_v.
```

In particular k_v is odd. For t_v=0 the upper bound forces tau_v>=1; for every t_v the lower bound forces tau_v<=3-t_v. The possible local tuples (t,tau,k) are

```
(0,1,5), (0,2,3), (0,3,1),
(1,0,5), (1,1,3), (1,2,1),
(2,0,3), (2,1,1), (3,0,1).
```

Summing tau over low vertices gives3T, so the empty-support counts imply T>=14 for h1, T>=9 for the h3 pair profile, and T>=8 for the h3 triple profile. Combined with the upper bounds, the necessary intervals are14..45,9..38,8..38 respectively. This is not a claim that every value in an interval is realizable. ## Arithmetic applicability: no exclusion yet

Modulo2, a zero-diagonal symmetric matrix of even order has square characteristic polynomial. Applying this to C gives

```
psi_h mod2 = (x²+x+1) u(x)²,
deg u = 23-h.
```

The prior `H7_CHARACTERISTIC_SQUARE_MODULE_AUDIT.md` also warns that repeated characteristic factors over GF(2) do not force additional ordinary kernel vectors: a nonsemisimple primary block can account for the multiplicity. No extra eigenvector is inferred here.

The displayed leading coefficients are compatible: the next-to-leading coefficient of u is1 for h1 and0 for h3. This partial parity check supplies no contradiction.

The Gram matrix of the low-space vectors consisting of B's rows and 1_l has determinant

```
7^(h-1) (343-22h-h²),
```

namely320 for h1 and13132 for h3. The complementary rational form K has determinant squareclass5 or67, respectively. The verifier constructs explicit 0/1 B matrices for all three incidence profiles and checks this Gram matrix directly. Therefore representability of this forced Gram block alone, including over Q_2 or Z_2, cannot exclude either profile: it already has an explicit integral representation. A useful Hasse/2-adic obstruction must also consume constraints on C, D, or a fully specified residual spectrum. No such full spectrum is supplied here.

The banked Hoffman parity theorem assumes even constant row sum. Mixed-degree A does not meet that premise. For the induced h1 graph F, codex-sol-2 supplied the following valid application: every6-regular C4-free connected component has at least31 vertices, since its30 nonbacktracking length-two endpoints are distinct and different from the center. ### Full-Gram mod4 redundancy (review #1578 PASS)

A stronger applicability statement replaces the earlier missing-polynomial warning for the *full* Gram polynomial. Let p(z)=det(I+zA). Every odd principal minor of a symmetric zero-diagonal integer matrix is even: modulo2 an odd-order alternating matrix is singular. Thus p(z)=E(z²)+2zO(z²) with integer E,O and E(0)=1. Multiplying p(z)p(-z) and substituting z²=-t yields the exact identity

```
det(I+tA²) = E(-t)² + 4t O(-t)².
```

Therefore the full Gram determinant polynomial is automatically a square modulo4 and has an integer formal-power-series square root by the banked mod4 square-series theorem. In the displayed q7 factorization, the already-required psi parity makes char_A modulo2 equal x times a square, which enforces exactly this odd-coefficient divisibility for the reversed polynomial p. Consequently this full-Gram test cannot further reject a candidate that has already passed that adjacency coefficient parity gate. This is a conditional algebraic implication; it does not assert existence of any complete residual polynomial, spectrum, or graph.

`verify_q7_full_gram_square_redundancy.py` checks the exact determinant identity on five integer symmetric zero-diagonal control matrices, including triangles and signed weights, and checks the first12 formal-root coefficients by an independent integer recurrence. The nonzero-diagonal control A=[1] fails root integrality at degree1, showing why the zero-diagonal premise cannot simply be removed. These controls are not a proof of the general statement or q7 survivors; the general proof is the determinant argument above.

The conclusion concerns only det(I+tA²). It does **not** settle mod4 tests on individual spectral sectors, compressed Gram matrices, or independently proposed Gram data without an adjacency-polynomial lift. Those tests require their own exact hypotheses. Substituting only the forced cubic for the unknown full Gram polynomial remains invalid.

## Defect components (review #1580 PASS)

Let S be a connected component of the low defect graph D. Put n=|S|, let k_i be the number of its vertices adjacent to high i in A, and let r_i count high-pair intersections contained in S and incident to i. Summing DB^T=J-B^T over S gives

```
n = 7k_i - sum_(v in S, i in support(v)) t_v = 6k_i-r_i.
```

For h3, each r_i lies in0..2; congruence modulo6 therefore makes all three r_i equal. The selected pair intersections form a regular simple graph on three high vertices, of degree0 or2. Consequently all shared support lies in one distinguished component with n=6k-2. Every other component has n=6k and contains only empty and singleton support. In all these components k is even: sum of D degrees is6n-3k and is even.

In a component with only empty/singleton support there are3k empty vertices. Each has three neighbors of singleton support, hence the induced empty graph is3-regular. Thus k>=2. In the pair-support profile the distinguished component cannot have k=2: it would contain the three pair vertices and no singleton, and a pair vertex would have no possible D-neighbor carrying its missing high label. Thus its k>=4. In the triple profile its k>=2.

Let c be the number of D components. Each component has the same strictly positive eigenvector a1-t for rho=a-1, so rho has multiplicity c. The rho eigenspace orthogonal to the global vector a1-t lies in K: it is orthogonal both to the other eigenvector in span(1,t) and to B^T(1-perp), whose D eigenvalue is-1. On that (c-1)-dimensional space, C² has eigenvalue b=6-rho=(7-sqrt37)/2. The polynomial x^4-7x²+3 is irreducible over Q. Integer char_C therefore has equal multiplicities at +sqrt(b) and -sqrt(b), so c-1 is even. Combining this with the component size counts leaves:

| H3 incidence profile | necessary D component sizes |
|---|---|
| Three pair supports | 46, or12+12+22 |
| One triple support | 46, or12+12+22, or10+12+24 |

These are necessary partitions, not constructions. For h1, the analogous equation is n=6k with k even and k>=2; summing k=8 permits48,12+36,24+24,12+12+24,12+12+12+12. The analogous quartic x^4-7x²+1 factors, so the preceding odd-component-count conclusion is not asserted for h1. More concretely, for h1 the relation a²-7a+1=0 gives b=7-a=((8-a)/3)² in Q(a). A scalar operator ((8-a)/3)I is therefore selfadjoint with square bI for every Gram form. The component-form conic obstruction used below for h3 cannot alone eliminate an h1 partition; this is not a graph construction.

A related parity condition holds for h1 and h3: the number of bipartite D components is even. Indeed -rho has precisely that multiplicity; on its eigenspace C²=6+rho. The polynomial x^4-17x²+(60+h) is irreducible for h=1,3, forcing equal multiplicities of the positive and negative C square roots. In particular a connected D is nonbipartite. These irreducibility checks were performed exactly with SymPy; graph spectral arguments remain paper deductions pending review and reconciliation with prior component work.
