# A6 high pairing, empty pair hosts and monotone singleton rows

Assume a validated a6 E/S projection: seven empties, eleven double-empty singleton vertices of S-degree one, and three single-empty singleton vertices of S-degree three. Accepted singleton-incidence lower bounds imply three mixed highs and four double/double highs. Name the first three highs by the ordered single-empty vertices. Match each to a distinct double-empty vertex, then partition the remaining eight double-empty vertices into four unordered pairs. Name those four highs by the increasing least element of their pairs. This covers all high labellings up to free renaming, with 11*10*9*105 = 103950 raw pairings.

Every paired singleton pair must have disjoint E/S neighbour sets, exactly the condition preventing a new C4 when its common high is added. Mixed pairs must additionally be nonedges by 2096. This nonedge restriction is not imposed on double/double pairs, whose high has singleton-empty incidence four.

At an empty e of F-degree d, its 7-2d singleton neighbours now have distinct high colours. Partition the 2d missing colours into d pairs, giving 1, 1, 3 or 15 local matchings for d=0,1,2,3. A high pair can be assigned to at most one empty. These conditions are necessary and sufficient for inserting all 21 pair vertices, their high edges and the selected pair-empty incidences without creating a C4. The proof is the same common-neighbour case split as 2110: a pair/singleton high+empty overlap is prevented by missing colours; pair/pair high+empty overlap is prevented by the matching; duplicate pair hosts are forbidden; matched singletons have disjoint old neighbours. All H/E common-neighbour counts become exactly one.

Singleton residual rows use only pair vertices. The three single-empty singletons each need two pair neighbours partitioning four uncovered colours, hence three templates. The eleven double-empty singletons each need three pair neighbours partitioning six uncovered colours, hence fifteen templates. There are 174 templates in total. If a double/double singleton pair is adjacent, its known same-high neighbour already covers its own high colour; subtract that colour exactly as for every other known singleton neighbour.

For singleton s define B(s) as the union of F-neighbours of its empty hosts and empty hosts of its known S-neighbours. A candidate row is valid precisely when its selected nonzero pair-empty hosts avoid B(s) and are pairwise distinct. Zero hosts impose no restriction. This is the 2113 star-intersection proof: disjoint high supports cover all missing colours, and the only remaining intersections are empty-host intersections with known neighbours or between selected pairs. The argument depends on the local S-degree and support identity, not on every high being mixed.

During recursive assignment of empty hosts, represent each as-yet unassigned pair by host zero. Assigning a previously zero host can only invalidate candidate singleton rows; it cannot introduce one. Therefore, if any optimistic singleton domain is empty, every descendant assignment fails, and the branch can be rejected. A nonempty optimistic domain is only a necessary condition. At a leaf, surviving assignments still require full residual-edge checking.

No a6 family search or exclusion is asserted by this criterion. The forthcoming pass must use a reviewed complete projection, count all high-pairing/host-recursion and singleton-template operations, retain UNKNOWN and unvisited domains under its fixed limits, and never retry a capped domain.
