# Odd-subgroup corollary at N80

Assume G is C4-free, has80 vertices and minimum degree9. The accepted N80/F5 exclusion2214 together with2176 says every order3 automorphism fixes exactly two vertices. Accepted2181 says every order5 automorphism fixes no vertex. Accepted2207 and2182 say the3- and5-parts of |Aut(G)| have exponent at most1; the accepted prime-support and order7 results leave only primes2,3,5.

There is no automorphism of order15. Suppose g had that order. Then h=g^5 has order3 and k=g^3 has order5, and they commute. Hence k permutes the two-element set Fix(h): if hx=x, then h(kx)=k(hx)=kx. The induced permutation has order dividing5, and a permutation on two points has order1 or2. It is therefore the identity. Thus k fixes both vertices, contradicting the fixed-point-free order5 theorem.

Nor can an element have order divisible15, since a suitable power would have order15. Using the prime-support and Sylow exponent bounds, every odd-order automorphism has order1,3 or5.

More strongly every odd-order subgroup H of Aut(G) has order1,3 or5. Its order divides15. A group of order15 has exactly one Sylow5 subgroup (its number divides3 and is1 modulo5) and exactly one Sylow3 subgroup (its number divides5 and is1 modulo3). These normal subgroups commute, and their product is cyclic of order15, contradicting the preceding exclusion.

This does not imply that3 and5 cannot both divide |Aut(G)|: a group of even order can contain subgroups of orders3 and5 without containing a subgroup of order15. No bound on the2-part, solvability assertion, graph exclusion, or solution of Erdős85 is claimed. No new search is used.
