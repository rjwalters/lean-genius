# Problem: Law of Large Numbers for Dependent Random Variables

**Slug**: laws-of-large-numbers-oq-03
**Created**: 2026-03-30
**Status**: Active
**Source**: gallery-extension (from laws-of-large-numbers openQuestion #3)

## Problem Statement

### Formal Statement

**Ergodic Theorem (Birkhoff, 1931)**: Let $(X, \mathcal{B}, \mu, T)$ be a measure-preserving system with $T$ ergodic, and $f \in L^1(\mu)$. Then:
$$
\frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} f(T^k x) \to \int f \, d\mu \quad \text{a.e.}
$$

This generalizes the Strong Law of Large Numbers to dependent sequences generated by an ergodic transformation.

### Plain Language

The classical Law of Large Numbers says that averages of independent random variables converge to the expected value. But what if the variables are dependent? The Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem provides the answer for a natural class of dependent sequences: those generated by iterating a measure-preserving transformation. The time averages still converge, but to the space average.

### Why This Matters

- Extends probability theory to dynamical systems and time series
- Foundation for statistical mechanics (time averages = ensemble averages)
- Connects probability, ergodic theory, and dynamical systems
- Practical: real-world data is almost never truly independent

### Our Goal

Formalize the Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem (or a key component like the maximal ergodic lemma) as an extension of the SLLN, connecting probability and ergodic theory.

## Initial Thoughts

### Potential Approaches

1. **Maximal ergodic lemma first**: Prove the maximal inequality, which is the hard core
   - Why it might work: Well-structured, concrete inequality
   - Risk: Technically demanding in measure theory

2. **Mean ergodic theorem (von Neumann)**: Prove the L2 version first
   - Why it might work: Simpler (Hilbert space theory), may have Mathlib support
   - Risk: Less novel, weaker result

3. **SLLN as special case framing**: Start from the existing SLLN and show it's a special case of Birkhoff
   - Why it might work: Natural extension narrative
   - Risk: Still need the full ergodic theorem

### Key Difficulties

- The pointwise convergence in Birkhoff is technically harder than the L2 convergence in von Neumann
- Measure theory infrastructure in Mathlib may or may not be sufficient
- Maximal ergodic lemma requires careful measure-theoretic arguments

### What Would a Proof Need?

- Ergodic transformation definition (Mathlib has this)
- Maximal ergodic lemma
- Dominated convergence or other limit theorems
- Connection to the i.i.d. case via product measures
