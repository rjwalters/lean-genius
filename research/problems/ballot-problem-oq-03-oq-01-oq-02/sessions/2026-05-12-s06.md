## Structural diagnosis

In **case 1** (`c.1 < c'.1`, so `c'.2 < c.2`):

* **c'-row residual is always 0.**  In case 1, S57.3a's
  `gnwProb_zero_of_row_eq_c'_case1` (Helpers.lean 14722) gives
  `gnwProb μ c K (c'.1, s) = 0` for `s < c'.2`.  Such cells `(c'.1, s)`
  in `(μ\c').cells` become **corners of `μ\c'`** (the obstruction `c'`
  to their corner status was removed): their right neighbor
  `(c'.1, s+1)` either equals `c'` (removed) or is to its right
  in the now-shortened row, and their leg in `μ\c'` is empty if
  `c'.1+1 ∉ μ\c'`'s column-`s` (which holds in our test cases).
  Both `gnwProb μ c K (c'.1, s)` and `gnwProb (μ\c') c K (c'.1, s)`
  vanish; residual contribution is 0.

* **c'-column residual is concentrated at cells `(i, c'.2)`
  with `i < c'.1`.**  These are the **case-1 arm-class non-vanishing
  crossing cells** of S57.6 prep 1 (Helpers.lean 15243 partition).
  S65's `hookLength_at_arm_class_case1` (Helpers.lean ~5005) gives
  `h_μ(i, c'.2) = h_{μ\c'}(i, c'.2) + 1`.  The K-step recurrence
  difference at such cells produces a per-cell residual whose
  weighted contribution **equals `−(h_d − 2)` per cell** in all
  three test cases above.

* **Off-spine residual concentrates at cells `(i,j)` with
  `i < c'.1 ∧ j > c'.2`** (the "case-1 off-spine quadrant").
  These are cells in `c`'s arm (`c`'s row is `c.1 = 0` in our
  tests; arm is `(0, c'.2+1), …, (0, c.2)`).  Their per-cell
  weighted residual sums to `+(h_d − 2)` in all three tests.

The cancellation is between:

```
on-spine c'-column class: −(h_d − 2)        [single non-vanishing crossing y = (i, c'.2)]
off-spine c-arm region:    +(h_d − 2)        [cells in c's arm strictly right of c'.2]
```

In `(4,2)`, the off-spine c-arm cells `(0,2)` and `(0,3)` each
contribute `+1` weighted residual; in `(3,2)` and `(3,2,1)`, only
`(0,2)` (which is `c` itself) contributes.  The `+(h_d − 2)`
formula generalizes by `(h_d − 2) = (#off-spine c-arm cells)` in
the test cases (since in case 1 with `c.1 = 0`, the off-spine
c-arm cells are `(0, c'.2+1), …, (0, c.2)` numbering `c.2 − c'.2 = h_d − 2`).

(This `(h_d − 2)` interpretation is verified in our three tests
but not proven in general; the next analysis session should check
e.g. `(3,2,2)` with corners `(0,2)`, `(2,1)`, where there are
**two** c'-column non-vanishing crossings.)

## Implication for `F_side_identity_aligned`'s proof

The cleanest target remains the **unfiltered** statement (matching
the actual `.lean` lemma at line 15670).  S57.7 should NOT be
restated as an off-spine-filtered identity, because that statement
is strictly stronger and false.

A proof of `F_side_identity_aligned` can proceed via:

1. **Decompose the sum** over `(μ\c').cells` into three regions:
   c'-row cells (`x.1 = c'.1, x.2 < c'.2`), c'-column cells
   (`x.2 = c'.2, x.1 < c'.1`), off-spine cells
   (`x.1 ≠ c'.1 ∧ x.2 ≠ c'.2`).

2. **Show c'-row contribution vanishes** on both sides (S57.3a +
   the dual `gnwProb_zero_of_col_eq_c'_case1` for `(μ\c')`).

3. **Identify the c'-column residual** as
   `∑_{i < c'.1, (i,c'.2) ∈ μ\c'} (gnwProb μ c h_μ(i,c'.2) (i,c'.2) · (h_d-1)²
   − gnwProb (μ\c') c h'(i,c'.2) (i,c'.2) · h_d·(h_d-2))`.
   In the three test cases this equals `−(h_d − 2)`; in general
   the formula likely depends on the column profile of `μ` at
   column `c'.2`.

4. **Identify the off-spine residual** as the complementary sum,
   show it equals the negation of step 3.

S57.7 (corrected) is step 4 — and it requires a sum-level
combinatorial bijection, *not* per-cell equality.
