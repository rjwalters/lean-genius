### §2.3  Mechanism comparison — both `simp only [hN]` and `change ...; rw [hN]` close the goal

S76 §5.3 recommended `simp only [hN]` because `simp` can chain through
the `_ / r % r` / `_ % r` / `_ / (yBound cfg * (r * r))` / `_ / (r * r) % yBound cfg`
projections via Nat-arithmetic congruence rules.  PR #19264 chose the
strictly stronger `change <explicit-form>; rw [hN]` form which:

1. Uses `change` to commit the goal to the explicit projected
   equality (no `simp` reductions, no risk of over-simplification).
2. Applies `rw [hN]` directly on the exposed `canonCrossN` term.
3. For the `Fin`-valued `canonI`/`canonJ` cases (lines 2325-2332),
   prepends `apply Fin.ext` to reduce `Fin r`-equality to underlying
   `Nat`-equality before `change`.

Both forms close the goal; #19264's choice is more **legible** (the
projection shape is visible in the proof script) and more **stable**
under v4.27+ Mathlib upgrades (no dependence on `simp` chain rules
which may evolve).  S76's `simp only [hN]` would also work but
implicitly relies on Mathlib's `Nat.div_*` / `Nat.mod_*` simp lemmas
chaining cleanly.

For sites 2336/2338 (now 2343-2354 after the `simp [splitPosAt, ...]`
expansion to multi-line `show ...; unfold splitPosAt; rw [...]` form),
S76's `simp only [splitPosAt]; omega` recommendation was bypassed in
favour of `unfold splitPosAt; rw [hci', hc₀', hy₀']`.  The mechanic's
choice avoids `omega` (which would have been needed if `simp only`
left an arithmetic residual) by relying on the substituted hypothesis
chain to close the goal definitionally.  Both forms work; PR #19264's
is again more legible.
