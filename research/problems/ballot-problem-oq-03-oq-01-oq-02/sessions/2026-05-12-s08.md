## Structural diagnosis: walk-vanishing crossings

The cell `(1,1)` in `(4,3,2)` is a non-corner of both `μ` and
`μ\c'`. By S68's structural criterion (row length `> c'.2 + 1`),
it is NOT double-vanishing.  Yet:

* `gnwProb μ c K (1,1) = 0` because `H*((1,1)) = {(1,2), (2,1)}`
  in `μ`, both corners ≠ c, so the K=1 average is `(0+0)/2 = 0`
  and propagates stably.
* `gnwProb (μ\c') c K (1,1) = 0` because `H*'((1,1)) = {(1,2)}`
  in `μ\c'`, the only element is a corner ≠ c.

**General walk-vanishing criterion (for case 1).** A
c'-column cell `(i, c'.2)` with `i < c'.1` is walk-vanishing
on BOTH sides iff every strict-hook descendant lies in a
no-c-reachable subgraph.  In `(4,3,2)`, `(1,1)`'s strict hook
in `μ` consists of:
* `(1,2)`: corner of `μ` ≠ c, contributes 0.
* `(2,1)` = c': corner of `μ` ≠ c, contributes 0.
Removing c' eliminates `(2,1)` from `μ\c'` but `(1,2)` remains
a corner ≠ c.

**Diagnostic test.** The walk-vanishing criterion subsumes
S68's double-vanishing structural criterion: if a cell is a
corner of `μ\c'` (S68 double-vanishing), all its strict-hook
descendants are absent in `μ\c'` and the (μ\c')-side is 0;
the μ-side analysis requires checking the strict-hook
descendants reach c via μ.  Walk-vanishing is the strictly
broader category that captures cases like `(1,1)` in `(4,3,2)`.

**Implication.** S68's surviving conjecture
`c'-column residual = −|c-arm region|` assumed each
non-vanishing crossing (in the structural-vanishing sense)
contributes equally to the residual.  S69 shows this fails
when walk-vanishing crossings exist: they contribute 0
despite the structural criterion classifying them as
"non-vanishing".
